Anthropic

Nov 10–17Anthropic launches IPO roadshow: Nasdaq ticker “ANT”, $70–78 per share

Anthropic’s Nov 10 announcement says it has launched the roadshow for a proposed IPO of 825,000,000 Class A shares (801,351,354 from Anthropic, 23,648,646 from selling stockholders), with an expected price of $70.00–78.00 per share and a 30-day underwriter option for up to 123,750,000 more shares. It has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as “ANT”; the preliminary prospectus is available from Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. The registration statement is filed but not yet effective, so pricing and timing are not final; press reports say pricing is expected around Nov 18 with trading Nov 19. The Financial Times reported on Nov 10–11, citing unnamed sources, that bankers are weighing pricing above the range on strong demand, and Bloomberg reported that an earlier draft emailed to bankers carried different share and price figures later called inaccurate. Those reports are anonymously sourced and were seen only via search summaries. Bloomberg, citing unnamed people, reported on Nov 12 that orders were about six times the shares on offer; outlets citing a Sunday, Nov 15 Bloomberg report say orders were close to seven times (that Bloomberg article was not seen), and Bloomberg also reported existing backers would get priority in allocations. Reports describe a $26 billion private placement of non-voting shares at the IPO price alongside the offering; TSMC’s board approved up to $500 million of it on Nov 10, per a TSMC filing summarized by outlets. As of the morning of Nov 17 no change to the $70–78 range had been reported and the IPO had not priced; CNBC’s Nov 16 guide and Bloomberg say pricing is expected Wednesday evening, Nov 18, with Nasdaq trading from Thursday, Nov 19. One secondary outlet (FourWeekMBA) reports a third S-1 amendment on Nov 17 that keeps the range and adds Nasdaq’s listing approval; that filing was not seen on EDGAR. Talk of pricing above the range is commentary, not a decision. Bank of America’s CEO was quoted on Nov 16 saying the offering may push some smaller IPOs into 2027. Bloomberg says Nvidia’s roughly $10 billion share-purchase commitment is under scrutiny after Huang’s exit (see Nvidia).

Nov 13OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet so its “dots” agents can make purchases

Multiple outlets report that OpenAI turned on ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, Nov 13, for US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 or older with eligible US-issued cards. The feature was built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. Per the reports, OpenAI’s “dots” agents pay with a card-network token instead of the real card number, and users approve each purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint. Reportedly the Wallet works only inside dots conversations, and OpenAI has given no timeline for wider availability. OpenAI said agentic commerce is at an early stage and some sites may block such purchases. No OpenAI post was reviewed; seen via search summaries of press coverage.

Nvidia

Nov 11–17Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO

Nvidia announced on Nov 12 that Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately; a Form 8-K summary says the board accepted the resignation on Nov 11. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while remaining CFO, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the board’s first chair. Jones said operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap will continue without interruption, and the board will consider internal and external candidates for a permanent CEO. The announcement follows a Nov 11 Wall Street Journal report that the board was preparing to oust Huang; an unnamed source says the board decided that the night before, but Nvidia calls it a resignation. House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast said Huang agreed on Nov 11, after lawmakers threatened a subpoena, to testify before Thanksgiving, likely behind closed doors but recorded and transcribed; a committee letter had sought a transcribed interview on Dec 1. Bloomberg reported on Nov 15 that Select Committee on China chair John Moolenaar said the date was still being worked out with the Foreign Affairs and Intelligence committees; no date had been reported as set as of Nov 17. CNBC says shares fell as much as about 14% intraday on Nov 12 and closed down about 4.3%. Bloomberg reports Nvidia’s roughly $10 billion commitment to buy Anthropic shares alongside its IPO is now under scrutiny, though neither company has announced a change. A source told CNBC on Nov 16 that the board is not considering bringing Huang back and is focused on its probe, led by Wachtell Lipton, and is reportedly choosing a search firm; Trump said on Nov 16 he would welcome Huang back, and the Financial Times reported, citing unnamed sources, that Huang had discussed a comeback with allies. Shares fell about 4% on Monday, Nov 16 (to about $156, per one secondary outlet) as the Nasdaq dropped about 2.5% and chip stocks slid. Nvidia reports fiscal Q3 results today, Tuesday, Nov 17, after the close, its first under Kress, against guidance of about $108 billion in revenue (LSEG consensus about $109.1 billion revenue and $2.47 EPS); results were still pending when this snapshot was compiled, and no permanent CEO had been named. Seen via search summaries of the filing and press coverage; a reported “third affair” is unverified and omitted.

Nov 12–13Valar Atomics files first permit for reactor-and-data-center campus on Utah state land

The Salt Lake Tribune reported on Nov 12 that Valar Atomics has submitted its first permit application for a campus of small reactors paired with data centers. The site is about 640 acres of Utah state trust land near Wellington in Carbon County, with 40–120 reactors per reports. The application adds a natural-gas plant to run operations until the reactors come online and then serve as backup, which a state senator called a “bait-and-switch.” The Daily Caller (Nov 13) reports that the project won local approval for up to 120 reactors. Another search found no record of a Carbon County vote, so that approval is unconfirmed. A separate, much larger proposal on federal BLM land (about 456 reactors, about 9.6 GW) remains under review. Seen via search summaries only.
