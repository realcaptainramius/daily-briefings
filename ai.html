Nov 20–21Anthropic mathematician posts a claimed Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis as Lean code

Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge released on Nov 20 a Lean 4 archive of about 175,000 files that he says is a machine-checked proof of the Riemann hypothesis and the Generalized Riemann hypothesis, developed “working with Claude.” Per coverage, the Lean FRO and Mathlib communities said on Nov 21 they have strong confidence the formal proof checks and that its theorem matches Mathlib’s standard statement, and MIT’s Andrew Sutherland and Oxford’s James Maynard are quoted as highly confident. No human-readable proof has been published, Stanford’s Brian Conrad criticized releasing a proof its author does not fully understand, and one paper argues the formal statement is trivial. This is a claim that has not been peer reviewed; reports differ on how much credit Claude gets. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 16Anthropic shortens its vulnerability-disclosure deadlines to 30 and 45 days from 2027

Anthropic said it will cut its coordinated vulnerability disclosure windows for flaws its models find, effective for reports filed on or after Jan 1, 2027: high- and critical-severity flaws will be published 30 days after reporting and all others after 45 days, down from the 90-day norm. It will also escalate to an external coordinator after 15 days of maintainer silence (from 30) and wait 30 days after a patch (from 45) before releasing full technical details. Open reports keep their old deadlines, though serious flaws already past 90 days get 30 more days. Anthropic cites AI finding bugs faster than they can be fixed and says hard cases such as critical infrastructure will get negotiated timelines. Seen via search summaries of Anthropic’s post and coverage.

Nov 22OpenAI chief scientist proposes industry-wide limits on model capability; Altman endorses

In an essay titled “Perplexity and Intelligence” published Sunday, Nov 22, chief scientist Jakub Pachocki proposed that AI developers agree on common limits on increasingly powerful models, using perplexity as a shared yardstick, and commit not to cross them until their ability to monitor model behavior reaches a concrete milestone.
