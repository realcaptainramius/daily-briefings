Anthropic

Oct 7Anthropic launches Claude Haiku 5.5 with up to 90% lower API prices

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5, a small model with a 1M-token context window and up to 128K output tokens. Reported API pricing is two-tier: $0.10 per million input and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100K tokens (about 90% below Haiku 4.5), and $0.50/$2.50 above 100K. Anthropic puts the average workload saving at about 75% because an updated tokenizer uses more tokens for the same text (one outlet says roughly 30% more). Figures come from press coverage rather than Anthropic’s pricing page, which was not checked directly.

OpenAI

Oct 7–8OpenAI rolls out GPT-6 and “Intelligent UI” in ChatGPT

Secondary coverage says OpenAI began rolling GPT-6 out in ChatGPT on Oct 7 with paid plans first and Free and Go tiers scheduled for Oct 8; paid plans use GPT-6 Sol and Free/Go use GPT-6 Luna. Intelligent UI lets responses mix prose with interactive elements such as buttons, forms, charts and diagrams, rendered progressively as the model generates them. The change applies to the Chat experience only; models behind Work and Codex are unchanged, and Enterprise access depends on admin settings. Details come from press summaries, not OpenAI’s own post, which was not reviewed.

Search Engine Journal · Let’s Data Science · TestingCatalog
Oct 6OpenAI publishes 722 AI-written math manuscripts from an unreleased internal model

OpenAI released 722 manuscripts, grouped into 372 result families across 17 fields, in a public GitHub repository under an Apache 2.0 license; they were produced by an internal model that has not been released, from roughly 4,000 problems posed. Lean formal proofs cover the main result of 162 of the papers, and OpenAI acknowledges results without a formal check may contain errors. An outside advisory group said its advice was not an endorsement, and critics including a group of Fields Medal winners have objected to mass-producing results. The 722 figure should not be read as 722 independently verified discoveries.

Oct 7Google opens SynthID Detector to everyone worldwide

Google announced on Oct 7 that SynthID Detector is now publicly available globally, in English, to check images, video and audio for hidden AI watermarks. It detects marks from Google and supported partners including OpenAI, Nvidia and Kakao; support for Apple-made media is planned but not yet available. The portal had previously been limited to a small group of testers such as journalists and researchers. Google cautions that unmarked content may still be AI-generated, and one hands-on test found results varied with how media was submitted.

Microsoft

Oct 7Microsoft and Nvidia launch Surface Laptop Ultra on RTX Spark, with Windows 11 changes for agentic AI

At its Windows and Surface event in San Francisco on Oct 7, Microsoft unveiled the 15-inch Surface Laptop Ultra built on Nvidia’s RTX Spark platform, with up to 128GB unified memory and up to one petaflop of FP4 AI performance. Pricing starts at $2,599 (a separate Surface RTX Spark Dev Box is $5,999); preorders are open with shipping Oct 16, and Asus, Dell, Lenovo, HP and MSI RTX Spark PCs follow the same day. Nvidia’s performance comparisons against Apple’s M5 Pro are the company’s own preliminary figures. The launch coincided with Windows 11 changes aimed at agentic AI; which executives presented on stage was not confirmed in the coverage reviewed.

Nuclear Power for AI

Oct 6Google signs 3.59 GW of power deals with Constellation Energy, including 890 MW from nuclear uprates

Reuters-sourced reports say Google and Constellation announced on Oct 6 a package totaling about 3,590 MW in the PJM grid. It combines a 20-year nuclear power purchase agreement for roughly 890 MW tied to upgrades at existing Constellation reactors in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey (first output expected in 2028) with a separate roughly 2,700 MW supply agreement that is not tied to a specific generation source. Constellation says it will invest more than $4.3 billion in its fleet, and the companies also agreed to a technology partnership using Google AI tools in plant operations. Analysts note only about a quarter of the volume is new nuclear output and that it comes mainly from uprates, not new reactors. Sources differ on contract length for the 2,700 MW portion (one says 15 years). This was missed in earlier briefings; the NANO Nuclear/IP3/Cybernetic Intelligence letter of intent (Sept 28) is also already covered.

Space (AI-relevant)

Oct 1Firefly signs Starcloud to demonstrate AI computing in lunar orbit

Firefly Aerospace announced a commercial payload agreement under which Starcloud’s SC-1L computing payload will fly on Firefly’s Elytra orbital vehicle to validate capabilities for a future data center around the Moon. It is slated for Firefly’s third lunar mission, targeted no earlier than 2028, where Elytra serves as transfer vehicle and communications relay for a Blue Ghost lander headed to the Gruithuisen Domes under NASA’s CLPS program.
