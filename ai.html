Sep 30Anthropic makes Claude for Government generally available to federal and state agencies

Anthropic announced on Sept 30 that Claude for Government is out of beta (public beta began in July) and generally available to federal and state agencies, delivered through a FedRAMP High authorized environment. Agencies pay no seat fees; usage is bought in fixed increments under a hard not-to-exceed cap. Administrators get department-level controls, single sign-on, audit logs and two-person approval for sensitive operations. The Claude Code CLI and Claude for Microsoft 365 are in early access.

OpenAI

Oct 1OpenAI parts ways with three safety researchers over alleged mishandling of sensitive information

The Wall Street Journal reported, and TechCrunch relayed, that OpenAI parted ways with three members of its safety team who allegedly shared confidential company information with a third-party AI safety organization. An OpenAI spokesperson said it parted ways with three individuals for violating policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information. The WSJ later named the three as Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni; TechCrunch has not independently confirmed their identities, and the organization and information involved were not disclosed.

Google / DeepMind

Sep 30Google DeepMind launches Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model starting with vetted cyber defenders

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on Sept 30, a new top-tier model aimed at long-horizon software engineering, defensive cybersecurity and enterprise knowledge work; it can generate up to 1 million tokens in one run versus 64,000 for earlier Gemini generations. Rollout begins with vetted cyber defenders through Google’s Fairwind Program, with paid API tiers and AI Ultra subscribers next and no dates given; it is not available to free users. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens. Google says Argon scores above OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic’s models on a range of benchmarks (company claim). Google skipped a planned Gemini 3.5 Pro.

Space (AI-relevant)

Oct 1Firefly signs Starcloud to demonstrate AI computing in lunar orbit

Firefly Aerospace announced a commercial payload agreement under which Starcloud’s SC-1L computing payload will fly on Firefly’s Elytra orbital vehicle to validate capabilities for a future data center around the Moon. It is slated for Firefly’s third lunar mission, targeted no earlier than 2028, where Elytra serves as transfer vehicle and communications relay for a Blue Ghost lander headed to the Gruithuisen Domes under NASA’s CLPS program.
