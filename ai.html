Anthropic

Nov 10–11Anthropic launches IPO roadshow: Nasdaq ticker “ANT”, $70–78 per share

Anthropic’s Nov 10 announcement says it has launched the roadshow for a proposed IPO of 825,000,000 Class A shares (801,351,354 from Anthropic, 23,648,646 from selling stockholders), with an expected price of $70.00–78.00 per share and a 30-day underwriter option for up to 123,750,000 more shares. It has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as “ANT”; the preliminary prospectus is available from Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. The registration statement is filed but not yet effective, so pricing and timing are not final; press reports say pricing is expected around Nov 18 with trading Nov 19. The Financial Times reported on Nov 10–11, citing unnamed sources, that bankers are weighing pricing above the range on strong demand, and Bloomberg reported that an earlier draft emailed to bankers carried different share and price figures later called inaccurate. Those reports are anonymously sourced and were seen only via search summaries. No change to the terms had been reported as of Nov 13; Bloomberg says Nvidia’s roughly $10 billion share-purchase commitment alongside the IPO is under scrutiny after Huang’s exit (see Nvidia).

Nov 9Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2

Anthropic released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, the same underlying model: Fable carries cybersecurity and biology safeguards and is generally available (including AWS, Google Cloud and Azure), while Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted organizations through Cyber and Life Sciences verification programs, plus Claude Enterprise customers via Claude Security. A secondary report of a 20% Fable price cut was not confirmed by the announcement, which does not state prior prices.

Nov 12Google launches Gemini 4 Hydrogen, its fast model replacing Gemini 3.8 Flash

Google’s blog announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen, the successor to Gemini 3.8 Flash and the first fast model without the “Flash” name. Google says it completes tasks 2.5x faster than 3.8 Flash. Benchmark figures are vendor or third-party reported; seen via search summaries of Google’s posts and press coverage.

Nvidia

Nov 11–12Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO

Nvidia announced on Nov 12 that Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately; a Form 8-K summary says the board accepted the resignation on Nov 11. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while remaining CFO, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the board’s first chair. Jones said operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap will continue without interruption, and the board will consider internal and external candidates for a permanent CEO. The announcement follows a Nov 11 Wall Street Journal report that the board was preparing to oust Huang; an unnamed source says the board decided that the night before, but Nvidia calls it a resignation. Three House committees reportedly asked Huang to sit for a closed-door interview on Dec 1. CNBC says shares fell as much as about 14% intraday on Nov 12 and closed down about 4.3%. Bloomberg reports Nvidia’s roughly $10 billion commitment to buy Anthropic shares alongside its IPO is now under scrutiny, though neither company has announced a change. Nvidia reports earnings Nov 17. Seen via search summaries of the filing and press coverage; a reported “third affair” is unverified and omitted.

Nov 8–10Nvidia board opens probe after reports CEO was target of alleged Chinese intelligence effort; stock falls two more days

Nuclear Power for AI

Nov 12Centrus to supply HALEU fuel for Oklo’s Meta-linked Ohio reactor campus

World Nuclear News reports Oklo signed multi-year contracts with Centrus Energy for HALEU fuel from Centrus’s Piketon, Ohio enrichment plant, with deliveries starting in 2029. The fuel covers phase 1 of Oklo’s planned 1.2 GW Ohio campus serving Meta data centers, and Meta’s prepayment to Oklo helps fund the purchase. No dollar value was in the summary seen; the article itself was not reviewed.

World Nuclear News
Nov 9NRC issues construction permit for Dow/X-energy Long Mott Xe-100 plant

The NRC issued a construction permit for four X-energy Xe-100 high-temperature gas-cooled reactors at Dow’s Seadrift, Texas site. It is the first HTGR construction permit in about 60 years, the first on an industrial site and the third construction permit of 2026; operation is expected in the early 2030s. The project powers an industrial site, not data centers; its AI link is indirect, through Amazon-backed X-energy’s separate data-center SMR plans. Seen via search summaries.
