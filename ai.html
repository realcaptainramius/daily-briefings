Anthropic’s Nov 10 announcement says it has launched the roadshow for a proposed IPO of 825,000,000 Class A shares (801,351,354 from Anthropic, 23,648,646 from selling stockholders), with an expected price of $70.00–78.00 per share and a 30-day underwriter option for up to 123,750,000 more shares. Anthropic (Nov 10)
Nov 9Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2

Anthropic released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, the same underlying model: Fable carries cybersecurity and biology safeguards and is generally available (including AWS, Google Cloud and Azure), while Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted organizations through Cyber and Life Sciences verification programs, plus Claude Enterprise customers via Claude Security. Pricing is $8/$40 per million input/output tokens, with an optional fast mode at $16/$80. Anthropic reports 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 and 69.9% on FrontierSWE v2, and says outputs are up to 30% faster than the 5.1 models; all benchmarks are vendor-reported and the post says outside organizations did not review it. A secondary report of a 20% Fable price cut was not confirmed by the announcement, which does not state prior prices.

Nvidia

Nov 8–10Nvidia board opens probe after reports CEO was target of alleged Chinese intelligence effort; stock falls two more days

Per NBC, CBS and ABC summaries, Nvidia’s board said on Sunday, Nov 8 that a committee of independent directors with outside counsel will investigate reporting that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort; the board said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and Huang remains CEO and apologized to staff for an affair. The New York Times reportedly identified the woman as a Chinese citizen whom US intelligence believes gathered information for Beijing; China called the allegation unsubstantiated. On Nov 9 President Trump said the FBI is looking into it, and a shareholder class action was filed. Shares fell about 4% on Nov 9 and about 5.5% on Nov 10 (close reported near $168.80) after a further unverified report about chip diversions to China. Allegations are unproven; only search summaries were seen.
