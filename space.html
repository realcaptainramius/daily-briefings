Firefly Aerospace

Oct 1Firefly signs Starcloud to demonstrate AI computing in lunar orbit

Firefly announced a commercial payload agreement to fly Starcloud’s SC-1L computing payload on its Elytra orbital vehicle to validate the core capabilities of a future data center around the Moon. It is onboarded to Firefly’s third lunar mission, targeted no earlier than 2028, where Elytra acts as transfer vehicle and communications relay for a Blue Ghost lander bound for the Gruithuisen Domes under NASA’s CLPS initiative.

Blue Origin

Sep 30Blue Origin says its first outside funding round is oversubscribed and not yet closed

CNBC reported on Sept 30 that CEO Dave Limp said the company’s first-ever outside capital raise is oversubscribed and still open. Secondary outlets citing the Wall Street Journal put the raise at about $10 billion at a roughly $140 billion valuation, with Coatue leading with about $4 billion; those figures were not verified against a primary source.

CNBC · Quartz
Sep 29FAA publishes draft environmental assessment to raise New Glenn cadence at SLC-36 from 12 to 50 launches a year

The FAA published a draft environmental assessment of Blue Origin’s proposal to modify vehicle operator license VOL 24-133 and lift the New Glenn cap at Cape Canaveral’s Launch Complex 36 from 12 to 50 launches a year. New Glenn itself remains grounded after the May 28 static-fire explosion, with return to flight still targeted before year’s end.

SpaceX

Oct 5–6SpaceX scrubs Oct 5 launch of 21 SDA data transport satellites; retry set for Oct 6

The Falcon 9 launch of the Space Development Agency’s T1TL-A mission, 21 Northrop Grumman-built satellites, planned from SLC-4E for 1:17 a.m. PT Oct 5, scrubbed shortly before liftoff on an automated abort. Per search-result summaries the vehicle and payload are in good health and a new attempt is planned for Oct 6, on a booster flying for the sixth time. Launch outcome not confirmed at compile time.

Spaceflight Now
Oct 1SpaceX launches NASA’s Crew-13 to the ISS on the fastest Dragon trip yet

A Falcon 9 and Dragon lifted off at 11:10 a.m. EDT Oct 1 from Cape Canaveral SLC-40 carrying NASA’s Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, CSA’s Joshua Kutryk and Roscosmos’ Sergey Teteryatnikov. The flight to the station took about eight hours, the fastest Crew Dragon trip to the ISS to date, for a roughly six-month stay. The first-stage booster landed back at Cape Canaveral.
