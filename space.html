Firefly Aerospace

Nov 10–11Firefly reports Q3 revenue of $110.7M and record $1.7B backlog; Alpha Block 2 debut set for Q1 2027

Firefly’s Q3 2026 release reports revenue up 259% to $110.7 million, a record $1.7 billion backlog and 2026 revenue guidance of $440–450 million (low end raised), with a wider loss per transcript coverage. On the call, management said Alpha demand exceeds near-term rocket supply, Alpha Flight 8 (first Block 2) is scheduled for Q1 2027, Blue Ghost Mission 2 is ahead of its 2027 plan, and it signed SSC Space for two Alpha launches from Esrange in Sweden and Synspective for up to 10 Alpha launches through 2030. Shares were reported up about 10% intraday on Nov 11, and B. Riley (Buy, target raised to $54 from $40) and Barclays (Equal-Weight, target $35) raised price targets. Seen via search summaries of the release, transcripts and market coverage.

Nov 6–7AST SpaceMobile signs engine deal worth more than $1.3B with Firefly for its planned heavy-lift rocket

AST SpaceMobile and Firefly announced a deal under which Firefly supplies 36 engines (27 Miranda and 9 Vira) for the first three vehicles of AST’s planned heavy-lift rocket, with AST also getting a perpetual license to build the engines in its own US facility plus engineering support. Reports describe the payment structure differently (one cites $800 million upfront split between cash and AST stock); first flight is targeted for 2028 and neither engine has flown on an orbital mission. Firefly shares rose sharply on Nov 6, with reported gains varying widely. Seen via search summaries of Spaceflight Now, Benzinga and a Firefly 8-K exhibit.

Blue Origin

Nov 5–7Blue Origin reported to push New Glenn return to flight to January 2027

Multiple outlets (The Next Web, NewsBytes, Gagadget, New Space Economy dated Nov 7) report that a memo from CEO Dave Limp tells staff the next New Glenn launch slips from December 2026 to January 2027, blaming the rebuild of Launch Complex 36 at Cape Canaveral and bad weather. The pad was damaged in a May 28, 2026 static-fire explosion and is the company’s only New Glenn pad. No specific launch date was given, and the next flight is reported to carry Amazon Leo satellites; a Blue Moon MK1 lander flight is listed for Q1 2027 and could slip. An earlier Oct 29 SatNews report of a November target is superseded. Seen via search summaries only; no Blue Origin statement was reviewed.

The Next Web · NewsBytes · New Space Economy (Nov 7) · Reuters via US News (Nov 5)
Nov 10Blue Origin says redesigned BE-4 oxygen valve is qualified; GS1-3 investigation to close this month

Blue Origin’s Nov 10 “GS1-3 Hotfire Updates” post concerns the May 28 New Glenn static-fire explosion at LC-36 that destroyed booster GS1-3. Per a search summary of the post, the root cause was traced to the main oxygen valve on one BE-4 engine; a redesigned valve has passed qualification testing, is installed in engines for upcoming flights and is being rolled out fleetwide, and the company expects to close the investigation later this month. The post itself could not be fetched. Around the same time Blue Origin reportedly announced the Northstar LOX/LNG booster engine and a GS3 third stage, and asked the FAA to raise New Glenn’s licensed cadence to 50 launches a year; exact dates for those were not confirmed.

SpaceX

Nov 5–13Starship Flight 15 still targeted for Nov 13 dual-catch attempt; no FAA license seen as of this run

Launch-day coverage dated Nov 13 says Flight 15 (Booster 22, Ship 42) is targeted for Friday, Nov 13, with a 75-minute window opening at 5:45 p.m. Eastern (4:45 p.m. Central) from Starbase Pad 2. Ship 42 rolled to the pad on Nov 12. Starlust reports the target slipped one day from Nov 12, citing SpaceX launch VP Kiko Dontchev. The flight is reported to carry 26 Starlink V3 satellites, and SpaceX plans to catch the booster minutes after launch and the ship about 22.5 hours later, around Saturday afternoon. NASASpaceFlight reported on Nov 12 that the FAA had not yet published the license modification needed for the ship’s return over populated areas. Gwynne Shotwell has said SpaceX expects the license to cover return to launch site and tower catch for both stages. No license issuance, scrub or launch result had been reported as of this run, so the date is a target, not a confirmed launch. Seen via search summaries only.

Axiom Space

Nov 10Axiom signs MOU with Nitride Global on in-space semiconductor research; False Claims Act suit against Vast unsealed

Axiom announced an MOU with Nitride Global to research manufacturing ultrawide-bandgap semiconductor materials in orbit, building on a NASA SBIR project on aluminum nitride crystal growth. Separately, reports dated Nov 10 say a False Claims Act suit Axiom filed under seal in Houston federal court on June 19 against Vast Space, alleging Vast’s NASA mission-planning document used Axiom proprietary information, was unsealed after a Nov 4 order; the allegations are unproven and Vast’s response was not seen. Final proposals in NASA’s commercial station competition are reportedly due Dec 14. Seen via search summaries.
