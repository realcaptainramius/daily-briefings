Blue Origin

Nov 19Google picks New Glenn to launch Project Suncatcher AI-compute satellites, targeting early 2029

Google and Blue Origin announced a launch agreement for New Glenn to carry Project Suncatcher satellites to sun-synchronous orbit, targeted for the first quarter of 2029; it would be Google’s first dedicated heavy-lift launch for the orbital-compute project. Neither company gave the number of satellites or the contract value. Suncatcher is still a research effort; Google flew a prototype with Planet on an October SpaceX rideshare. New Glenn has not flown since May’s explosion, and its return to flight is targeted for January 2027, so the 2029 date is a plan, not a commitment. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 15–19ULA Vulcan carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites fails; preliminary data point to fuel supply, not Blue Origin’s BE-4 engines

United Launch Alliance’s Vulcan, powered by two Blue Origin BE-4 engines, failed about 10 minutes into flight on Sunday, Nov 15, losing 40 Amazon Leo satellites; it is ULA’s first outright mission failure. ULA first said early data pointed to first-stage underperformance, and on Nov 18 said preliminary indications are that the low booster thrust resulted from an inadequate fuel supply from the vehicle to the BE-4 engines, not from the engines themselves, which appear to have operated as planned; Nov 19 coverage adds that the six Northrop Grumman solid rocket boosters performed normally and quotes ULA saying this “is not a final cause.” The finding is preliminary, the investigation with the FAA is ongoing, and Vulcan remains grounded. Before the flight, ULA said the engines had been retrofitted with Blue Origin’s fix for the oxygen-valve problem behind May’s New Glenn explosion. Blue Origin says it is supporting ULA’s investigation, and CEO Dave Limp reportedly said updates should come from ULA. No change to New Glenn’s January 2027 return-to-flight target has been reported, searches found no further ULA statement through Nov 25, and no Vulcan return-to-flight date has been given. Seen via search summaries only. One outlet’s headline claiming ULA attributed the failure to the BE-4s conflicts with ULA’s Nov 18 statement.

Nov 24FT: SpaceX and Tesla name directors to lead possible merger talks; SpaceX shares fall about 5.7%

The Financial Times reported on Nov 24 that Tesla and SpaceX have each named board members to committees negotiating a possible all-stock merger: per the FT as relayed by others, Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung for Tesla, and Roelof Botha and Randy Glein for SpaceX, with Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs reportedly advising SpaceX and Evercore advising Tesla. People involved reportedly cautioned that the committees are not close to agreement. No deal has been announced, and any merger would need Tesla shareholder and regulatory approval. SpaceX shares (SPCX) fell about 5.7% to $125.81 that day as other space and AI stocks also fell. Seen via search summaries of secondary coverage; the FT article was not read.

Nov 24Falcon 9 launches classified USSF-266 mission from Vandenberg

After slipping from Nov 23, a Falcon 9 launched the Space Force’s USSF-266 mission from SLC-4E at Vandenberg at about 8:08 a.m. Pacific (16:08 UTC) on Nov 24; the booster, reported as B1081 on its 29th flight, landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You. Nov 23Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX over Grok-generated sexual images, including of children

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones and Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed separate lawsuits under state consumer-protection laws on Monday, Nov 23. They allege that the Grok chatbot of SpaceX’s AI unit (formerly xAI) was launched without adequate safeguards, let users turn real photos into nude or sexual images, and that the company misled consumers about its protections. Virginia’s suit names SpaceX, X and the former xAI unit and cites a Center for Countering Digital Hate estimate that Grok generated millions of sexualized images between December 2025 and January 2026, more than 20,000 of them depicting children. Coverage describes these as the first state lawsuits after months of multistate demand letters. These are allegations only; SpaceX did not immediately comment. Seen via search summaries only; the complaints were not reviewed, and outlets differ on courts and relief sought.

Nov 22Falcon 9 launches NROL-136 spy-satellite mission 32 hours after USSF-317 from the same pad

A Falcon 9 launched NROL-136 for the National Reconnaissance Office from SLC-4E at Vandenberg at 4:23 a.m. Pacific (12:23 UTC) on Sunday, Nov 22, and the NRO said the mission succeeded. It was the 15th launch for the NRO’s proliferated reconnaissance architecture, built with SpaceX and Northrop Grumman; the payload’s details are classified. The booster, B1097 on its 14th flight per NASASpaceflight, landed at Landing Zone 4. Liftoff came about 32 hours after USSF-317 left the same pad, which NASASpaceflight says beat the SLC-4E turnaround record by about 23 hours and SpaceX’s fastest at any pad by about 13 hours. USSF-266 followed from the same pad on Nov 24 (see above). Seen via search summaries, including the NRO release.

Nov 19SpaceX’s AI unit denies liability in UK MP’s lawsuit over Grok images

Reuters reported on Nov 19 that SpaceX Super Intelligence (formerly xAI) denies liability in Labour MP Jess Asato’s High Court claim over sexualized Grok-generated images of her. In a written defence, the company argues that images reasonable people would see as “AI-generated, fictitious and implausible” cannot give her a reasonable expectation of privacy, and that it did not breach data-protection law because it “did not determine the content of the relevant users’ prompts.” Asato wants a court order requiring permanent technical measures to stop Grok producing such images of her. The court has not ruled. Seen via search summaries of Reuters syndication only.

Nov 20–21CRS-35 cargo Dragon leaves the ISS and splashes down off California

SpaceX’s CRS-35 cargo Dragon, launched Oct 13, undocked from the International Space Station at 12:05 p.m. Eastern on Friday, Nov 20, after poor weather delayed an earlier departure, and splashed down in the Pacific off California at 5:03 p.m. Eastern on Saturday, Nov 21, per Space.com. Nov 12–20Falcon 9 launches classified USSF-317 mission for the Space Force after repeated delays

After targets of Nov 12, 13, 14–15, 18 and 19 and an unexplained scrub on Thursday evening, Nov 19, a Falcon 9 launched USSF-317 from SLC-4E at Vandenberg at 8:20 p.m. PST on Friday, Nov 20 (04:20 UTC Nov 21), and SpaceX said the payload reached orbit. Booster B1100, on its 11th flight, landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You about 8.5 minutes after liftoff. Spaceflight Now’s updated headline describes the payload as satellites for the Space Force’s data network constellation, but the number of spacecraft and their purpose remain classified; that observers think they may be Starshield satellites is speculation. It was reported as the sixth in a series of Space Force missions assigned to SpaceX since August. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 17–24Apollo and Goldman price about $44B of notes for SpaceX’s leased Nvidia chips; shares dip below IPO price

Apollo Global Management and Goldman Sachs announced on Nov 20 that they had priced about $44 billion of senior and subordinated notes to finance Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs and related equipment that an Apollo affiliate will buy and lease to SpaceX, under the roughly $40 billion lease SpaceX disclosed in October; coverage says the deal was oversubscribed and upsized. SpaceX shares (SPCX) fell 7.7% to $127.79 on Friday, Nov 20, below the $135 June IPO price amid a broader AI sell-off, then rose about 4.4% to $133.35 on Monday, Nov 23, before falling about 5.7% on Nov 24 on the Tesla merger report (see above). Nov 12–20Ukraine reports new Starshield speed limits; Musk calls Zelensky’s claims false; incoming House Intelligence chair threatens SpaceX funding

Ukraine said on Nov 12 that SpaceX’s Starshield service again faced restrictions, an effective cap for terminals moving faster than about 100 mph (161 km/h) that hampers strike and interceptor drones. This follows an Oct 13–24 disruption. The Wall Street Journal reported that Musk ordered the limits after talks with President Trump. SpaceX denied imposing restrictions, a Nov 15 Ukrainian report says SpaceX attributed slowdowns to “technical issues,” Trump denied the White House ordered them, and Musk has said limits are decided by the US military and “ultimately POTUS.” Per WSJ-sourced reports from Nov 13–14, Democratic Sens. Jeanne Shaheen and Chris Coons asked Musk to preserve records and answer by Nov 30. On Nov 17, President Zelensky said the speed limit “in the temporarily occupied territories remains in place”; at the DealBook Summit on Nov 18, Musk called the claims false and said Zelensky is “not always truthful,” per CNBC on Nov 19. CNN reported on Nov 20 that Rep. Jim Himes, who becomes House Intelligence Committee chair in January, told Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton in a classified message that the committee could “ring fence” funding for certain SpaceX projects in the annual Intelligence Authorization Act until it gets answers, and separately asked Musk whether the October limits were SpaceX’s own decision. That is a threat, not an enacted restriction, and it is unclear whether Himes has the votes. The core claims are contested and rest on anonymous sourcing; no report of the limits being lifted had appeared as of Nov 23. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 17–18Cameron County commissioners decline to advance SpaceX tax break for $24B Starbase-area data center

The Cameron County Commissioners Court voted 3–2 on Nov 17 against scheduling a public hearing on SpaceX’s request for a 10-year, 100% property-tax abatement for a proposed $24 billion data center and gas-fired power plant near Starbase. That is a procedural vote, not a ruling on the abatement itself, but local coverage says it effectively ends the county-level request. SpaceX had warned it could move the investment elsewhere without the abatement. Per a Nov 18 local report, Texas environmental regulators issued SpaceX an air permit for the site the day before the vote, and a separate abatement request before the City of Starbase is reportedly set for a Dec 9 hearing. Separately, market-news outlets report CFO Bret Johnsen said at an Austin summit on Nov 17 that SpaceX could be “one of, if not the largest player in AI infrastructure” next year if it meets its targets; that is the company’s own projection. Seen via search summaries, mostly of local and market-news outlets.
