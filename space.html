Firefly Aerospace

Oct 1Firefly signs Starcloud to demonstrate AI computing in lunar orbit

Firefly announced a commercial payload agreement to fly Starcloud’s SC-1L computing payload on its Elytra orbital vehicle to validate the core capabilities of a future data center around the Moon. It is onboarded to Firefly’s third lunar mission, targeted no earlier than 2028, where Elytra acts as transfer vehicle and communications relay for a Blue Ghost lander bound for the Gruithuisen Domes under NASA’s CLPS initiative.

SpaceX

Oct 6–7FCC clears SpaceX’s next-gen direct-to-device constellation but defers its spectrum request; Oct 29 vote on new auction

Per Reuters, an FCC order on Tuesday (Oct 6) cleared the launch of SpaceX’s next-generation direct-to-device constellation but deferred SpaceX’s request for the 2020–2025 MHz band. The FCC said it will vote Oct 29 on whether to advance an auction of 25 MHz (1675–1695 and 2020–2025 MHz) usable for terrestrial 5G, direct-to-device service or both, with no definitive auction timeline. Amazon Leo is also developing direct-to-device service. The vote is an opening procedural step; outcome unknown.

Oct 7–8Crew-12 scheduled to splash down off California on Oct 8 after undocking from the ISS

NASA’s plan had the Dragon Freedom carrying Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, ESA’s Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos’ Andrey Fedyaev undock Oct 7 and splash down off California the morning of Oct 8 (listings give 11:25–11:34 a.m. ET), ending a mission that launched Feb 13. Every source reviewed was a pre-event schedule, so the splashdown itself is not confirmed here; undocking times also vary between listings.

Oct 6–7SpaceX reportedly in talks to raise about $40 billion in debt to buy Nvidia chips

Bloomberg, following the Financial Times, reported early-stage talks with banks and investors on roughly $40 billion of financing (reportedly about $10 billion of bank loans and $30 billion of investment-grade debt, with Apollo expected to lead) to buy Nvidia AI chips. Spaceflight Now · KSBY
Oct 1SpaceX launches NASA’s Crew-13 to the ISS on the fastest Dragon trip yet

A Falcon 9 and Dragon lifted off at 11:10 a.m. EDT Oct 1 from Cape Canaveral SLC-40 carrying NASA’s Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, CSA’s Joshua Kutryk and Roscosmos’ Sergey Teteryatnikov. The flight to the station took about eight hours, the fastest Crew Dragon trip to the ISS to date, for a roughly six-month stay. The first-stage booster landed back at Cape Canaveral.
