NDAA Status

FY27 National Defense Authorization Act: House/Senate progress, conference, and IT/cyber/space provisions.

No changeSenate still hasn't moved — standoff now in its twelfth week

Still no floor movement on the core stalemate: the House passed its FY27 NDAA (H.R. 8800, $1.15T) 216-212 back in July, largely on party lines. The Senate's own version (S. 4784) has been stuck since a July 14 cloture vote failed 50-46, with Democrats blocking debate over the still-unauthorized Iran conflict and the defense topline gap ($1.15T House vs. $1.14T Senate). Majority Leader Thune's motion to reconsider keeps the bill technically alive, but no renewed cloture attempt or other procedural step has been reported since, and no conference committee has been formally seated. Until the Senate acts, the bill's IT/cyber/space provisions — including the Space Development Agency dissolution language flagged previously — remain proposed text, not law.

Appropriations & Continuing Resolution

Defense appropriations bill status and government-funding/shutdown risk.

No changeFY27 opens on the CR — funding runs through Dec. 11

Fiscal 2027 began October 1 under the stopgap (H.R. 6500) Trump signed September 2, which funds the government at FY26 levels through December 11. On the defense bill specifically: the House Appropriations Defense Subcommittee's FY27 bill cleared full committee back on June 24 (34-27) but still hasn't had a floor vote, and Senate appropriators haven't marked up their version at all. No movement on either front was reported this week. Bottom line unchanged: no new-start IT/cyber/SATCOM money is fully appropriated, and won't be before December at the earliest — a long CR like this one typically freezes new starts and caps existing programs at prior-year funding profiles, which matters if your pipeline depends on a program getting its first tranche of FY27 dollars.

CRFB Appropriations Watch
No changeThree weeks after McConnell's return, still no reported Senate Defense markup

Sen. Mitch McConnell, chairman of the Senate Appropriations Defense Subcommittee, returned to the Capitol on September 14 after a three-month absence. That structural change to the "why is Senate Defense stuck" story hasn't yet produced a visible result: no markup has been announced or reported in the three weeks since, and questions about his capacity persist. With the Dec. 11 CR deadline now about ten weeks away, the window for a normal-order Senate markup, floor vote, and conference before the next funding cliff keeps narrowing.

Budget Priorities & Program Impacts

Reprogramming actions, budget requests, and funding moves affecting IT/cyber/space programs.

Oct 2026150+ FY27 new starts now frozen under the CR

With the fiscal year underway on a stopgap, Breaking Defense counts more than 150 new-start line items across the procurement and RDT&E budget documents that can't begin until full-year appropriations pass — including funding for new drones, the Golden Fleet program, hypersonics, and the Golden Dome missile-defense initiative. The CR also bars production-rate increases on existing hardware. For IT/cyber/space/SATCOM pipelines, a program without an FY26 funding line can't take its first FY27 dollars before December at the earliest, and a second CR would extend that. Separately, the Pentagon's ~$1.5B reprogramming request (including $600M to build a top-secret AI compute center on JWICS) was sent to Congress in July and no approval or denial has been reported.

Contract Vehicles & Procurement Policy

Major IT/cyber/SATCOM contract-vehicle timelines and the budget/policy context behind them.

Oct 7Megatron Industry Day is Wednesday, Oct. 7 — award still a mid-2027 event

No change to the schedule: the Air Force's official SAM.gov listing for the "Megatron" DAF Battle Network IDIQ (Notice ID FA861227RB001) lays out a schedule slower than "RFP expected this month" suggested. The draft SOW is out, but the draft RFP itself isn't targeted until late Q4 FY26 (roughly Oct–Dec 2026), a final RFP follows in mid-Q1 FY27 (~Jan–Feb 2027), and award is targeted for Q3 FY27 (~summer 2027). The program office is holding a virtual Industry Day on Oct. 7 (registration closed Oct. 5) to brief industry ahead of the draft RFP, with no one-on-one sessions planned given expected interest volume. Ceiling is still commonly cited around $4.5B+, but the Air Force hasn't confirmed a number. Worth resetting expectations if you were tracking this for a near-term bid — it's a mid-to-late-2027 award, not a fall 2026 one.

Bids due Oct 6JWCC UCM Core bids due tomorrow (Oct 6)

No change: DISA's $21.6B "UCM Core" hyperscaler cloud IDIQ (5-year base + 5-year option) has bids due October 6 — open to US-based hyperscalers, with a $100K minimum per contract. The companion "UCM Premier" vehicle for non-hyperscale and small-business/commercial-innovator cloud offerings still isn't expected to get a final solicitation until Q1 CY2027, with awards in Q3 2027 — that's the one to watch if you're not AWS/Google/Microsoft/Oracle.

Nextgov/FCW
Sep 3 / Sep 11CMMC Task Force report still not public; Rev. 3 class deviation codifies the Phase 2 suspension

The Task Force submitted its findings to the DoW Chief Information Officer around September 11 (roughly 60 days after the July 13 memo that launched the review and suspended Phase 2), after weighing more than 1,100 RFI comments on assessment scope, cloud/MSP treatment, and OT applicability. The report itself has not been made public; reporting still points to a public version in late September or October, but none was found as of this run. Phase 2 third-party assessment requirements — originally set to kick in November 10 — remain suspended in the meantime, and on September 3 DoW issued Rev. 3 of DARS Class Deviation 2026-O0025, which writes that suspension into DFARS Part 240 as binding acquisition rule until rescinded or incorporated into the DFARS. Whatever it recommends, the report is advisory only: DFARS 252.204-7012, NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2, SPRS scoring, and the annual affirmation all stay in force until an actual class deviation or rule change follows.
