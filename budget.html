NDAA Status

FY27 National Defense Authorization Act: House/Senate progress, conference, and IT/cyber/space provisions.

Nov 17–20Big Four negotiators say a compromise NDAA is close, with a House vote possible after Thanksgiving; no text released yet

The FY27 NDAA still isn't law. The House-passed H.R. 8800 (216-212, July 22) is the only chamber-passed text, and the Senate's S. 4784 never got past its failed July cloture vote. The four Armed Services leaders are negotiating a compromise directly. NDAA trackers citing a Nov. 19 POLITICO report say about "a couple dozen" issues remain, some of which need to go to leadership. HASC Chair Rogers reportedly said the House could vote as soon as lawmakers return from Thanksgiving. Ranking member Smith named right-to-repair for military equipment and a limit on defense-contractor stock buybacks as still in dispute. A POGO letter dated Nov. 19 urging conferees to keep right-to-repair confirms that the provision is still live. Rogers has reportedly also cast doubt on it surviving. Punchbowl (Nov. 17) calls the path to passage perhaps the thorniest ever, and Smith calls the topline "the big sticking point." Rogers has said he won't seek a waiver to stay the committee's top Republican next Congress. The timing claims above rest on tracker summaries of POLITICO reporting that couldn't be independently confirmed, so treat them as reported, not settled. No compromise text has been released, and the IT/cyber/space provisions, including the SDA dissolution language, are still proposals.

Appropriations & Continuing Resolution

Defense appropriations bill status and government-funding/shutdown risk.

Nov 16Senate Republicans release a ~$1.1T FY27 defense bill without a markup and fold $69B of reconciliation priorities into the base budget

On Monday, Nov. 16, the Senate Appropriations Committee posted its FY27 Defense bill text without holding a markup. It is the first Senate Defense bill this cycle, two months after Subcommittee Chair McConnell returned. Coverage puts the total at nearly $1.1T (some trackers list $1.072T), close to the House committee's $1.07T bill from June 24. The bill moves about $69B the White House had sought through reconciliation into regular discretionary funding. Golden Dome is the most visible item; details are under Budget Priorities below. Other reported items include $35.6B for air and missile defense, $6.2B for drone and counter-drone systems, and $3B for the Defense Autonomous Warfare Group. McConnell called it a "roadmap" for "generational investments." Incoming Appropriations Chair Murray criticized the defense increase, and she and Chair Collins are still billions apart on an overall topline for all 12 bills. The text is a Republican marker, not enacted funding. With Democrats taking both chambers in January, it serves mainly as an opening position for a final FY27 deal.

Nov 2026Dec. 11 CR deadline: no deal as of Nov. 18, and Senate Democrats are threatening to block any stopgap without Iran-war limits

FY27 is still running on the stopgap (H.R. 6500) that holds funding at FY26 levels through December 11, with a lapse starting December 12. Reporting as of Nov. 18 found no deal. Sen. Kaine is drafting war-ending language to attach to the next stopgap. Sens. Duckworth, Bennet, Sanders and Van Hollen have committed to block any short-term package that doesn't limit the Iran war, and Duckworth and Gillibrand want to cut off offensive operations while still funding defensive and intelligence work. Murray says "we're still going to have language stopping this war," and Jeffries has deferred to DeLauro on what's achievable. Sen. Ron Johnson and other Republicans reject tying funding to the war. A stopgap needs 60 Senate votes, so neither side can pass one alone. House members have left for Thanksgiving recess and come back with little more than a week before the deadline, and GOP leaders have also struggled with attendance from members who lost reelection (CNN, Nov. 20). Analysts still expect another stopgap into 2027. A long or full-year CR remains the scenario to plan for: it would mean no new starts and no rate increases for IT/cyber/SATCOM programs unless Congress grants anomalies. No reporting found a confirmed end date for the next CR.

Budget Priorities & Program Impacts

Reprogramming actions, budget requests, and funding moves affecting IT/cyber/space programs.

Nov 16–19$350B defense reconciliation all but dead, and Senate appropriators cut Golden Dome to $9.4B

Outgoing HASC Chair Rogers said at a Hudson Institute event that the $350B reconciliation package is "not going to happen." He said a smaller bill of roughly $100B might still have a path. Senate Appropriations Chair Collins and Defense Subcommittee Chair McConnell have also said they don't expect the package to pass. Axios (Nov. 19) reports House Republicans consider the Senate GOP's last-ditch $350B push dead on arrival without offsetting spending cuts. Deputy Defense Secretary Feinberg briefed senators behind closed doors to argue for it, and later reporting says the Pentagon is still trying to salvage a party-line bill. Golden Dome is where this lands hardest. Nearly all of its ~$17.5B FY27 request sat in reconciliation, with only about $398M in the base request. The Senate appropriations bill provides $9.4B, so the program gets base funding but at roughly half the request. Appropriators argued that relying on reconciliation threatens the program. Appropriators are also reportedly moving multiyear munitions buys into the base budget. The $67.1B defense share of the $87.6B Iran-war supplemental remains pending. For IT/cyber/space lines, the shift is toward a base-budget topline near $1.07–1.1T. Growth programs that depended on reconciliation money are the most exposed.

No change150+ FY27 new starts are still frozen under the CR; no reported disposition of the ~$1.5B reprogramming

More than 150 new-start line items in the FY27 procurement and RDT&E budget documents, counted by Breaking Defense in October, still can't begin under the CR. They include new drones, Golden Fleet, hypersonics and Golden Dome. Production-rate increases are also barred. The Pentagon's ~$1.5B reprogramming request, reported in September and including $600M for a top-secret AI compute center on JWICS, still has no publicly reported final disposition as of this run (Nov. 23).

Contract Vehicles & Procurement Policy

Major IT/cyber/SATCOM contract-vehicle timelines and the budget/policy context behind them.

Nov 2026DFARS rewrite: DoW profit policy may move from weighted guidelines to commercial benchmarks, with software/IT tied to SaaS margins

A Federal News Network analysis this month says the department's acquisition overhaul will move profit policy, contractor data requirements and business-systems rules through DFARS rulemaking. It says DoW may drop the long-standing DFARS weighted-guidelines profit method in favor of commercially benchmarked, sector-specific margins. Those benchmarks would inform negotiation objectives but would not act as caps. The changes trace to a mid-September Pentagon memo. Commentary on that memo says it ties software and IT-services profit to commercial software-as-a-service margins and R&D investment, and that it gave about 90 days to start rulemaking, which points to mid-December. The memo's exact date and the December start window come from secondary coverage and are unconfirmed. Until a proposed rule is published, DFARS 215.404-4 (weighted guidelines) remains in force. The change would reverse the department's 2023 Contract Finance Study, which recommended keeping weighted guidelines.
