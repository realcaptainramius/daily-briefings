NDAA Status

FY27 National Defense Authorization Act: House/Senate progress, conference, and IT/cyber/space provisions.

No changeSenate still hasn't moved — standoff now in its eleventh week

Still no floor movement on the core stalemate: the House passed its FY27 NDAA (H.R. 8800, $1.15T) 216-212 back in July, largely on party lines. The Senate's own version (S. 4784) has been stuck since a July 14 cloture vote failed 50-46, with Democrats blocking debate over the still-unauthorized Iran conflict and the defense topline gap ($1.15T House vs. $1.14T Senate). Majority Leader Thune's motion to reconsider keeps the bill technically alive, but there is no sign of a renewed cloture attempt on the floor calendar this week, and no conference committee has been formally seated. Until the Senate acts, the bill's IT/cyber/space provisions — including the Space Development Agency dissolution language flagged previously — remain proposed text, not law.

Appropriations & Continuing Resolution

Defense appropriations bill status and government-funding/shutdown risk.

No changeCR through Dec. 11 remains the operative funding mechanism

Still governed by the stopgap (H.R. 6500) Trump signed September 2, funding the government at FY26 levels through December 11. On the defense bill specifically: the House Appropriations Defense Subcommittee's FY27 bill cleared full committee back on June 24 (34-27) but still hasn't had a floor vote, and Senate appropriators haven't marked up their version at all. Nothing moved on either front this week. Bottom line unchanged from last week: no new-start IT/cyber/SATCOM money is fully appropriated, and won't be before December at the earliest — a long CR like this one typically freezes new starts and caps existing programs at prior-year funding profiles, which matters if your pipeline depends on a program getting its first tranche of FY27 dollars.

CRFB Appropriations Watch
No changeTwo weeks after McConnell's return, still no sign of a scheduled Senate Defense markup

Sen. Mitch McConnell, chairman of the Senate Appropriations Defense Subcommittee, returned to the Capitol on September 14 after a three-month absence. That structural change to the "why is Senate Defense stuck" story hasn't yet produced a visible result: no markup has been announced or reported in the two weeks since, and questions about his capacity persist. With the Dec. 11 CR deadline now just over ten weeks away, the window for a normal-order Senate markup, floor vote, and conference before the next funding cliff keeps narrowing.

Contract Vehicles & Procurement Policy

Major IT/cyber/SATCOM contract-vehicle timelines and the budget/policy context behind them.

CorrectionMegatron's RFP is an FY27 event, not a September one — official schedule is slower than first reported

Updating last week's item: the Air Force's official SAM.gov listing for the "Megatron" DAF Battle Network IDIQ (Notice ID FA861227RB001) lays out a schedule slower than "RFP expected this month" suggested. The draft SOW is out, but the draft RFP itself isn't targeted until late Q4 FY26 (roughly Oct–Dec 2026), a final RFP follows in mid-Q1 FY27 (~Jan–Feb 2027), and award is targeted for Q3 FY27 (~summer 2027). The program office is holding a virtual Industry Day on Oct. 7 to brief industry ahead of the draft RFP, with no one-on-one sessions planned given expected interest volume. Ceiling is still commonly cited around $4.5B+, but the Air Force hasn't confirmed a number. Worth resetting expectations if you were tracking this for a near-term bid — it's a mid-to-late-2027 award, not a fall 2026 one.

The companion "UCM Premier" vehicle for non-hyperscale and small-business/commercial-innovator cloud offerings still isn't expected to get a final solicitation until Q1 CY2027, with awards in Q3 2027 — that's the one to watch if you're not AWS/Google/Microsoft/Oracle.

Nextgov/FCW
Sep 11CMMC Reform Task Force report delivered internally — still not public

The Task Force submitted its findings to the DoW Chief Information Officer around September 11 (roughly 60 days after the July 13 memo that launched the review and suspended Phase 2), after weighing more than 1,100 RFI comments on assessment scope, cloud/MSP treatment, and OT applicability. The report itself has not been made public; a public version is still expected between late September and early October. Phase 2 third-party assessment requirements — originally set to kick in November 10 — remain suspended in the meantime. Whatever it recommends, the report is advisory only: DFARS 252.204-7012, NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2, SPRS scoring, and the annual affirmation all stay in force until an actual class deviation or rule change follows.
