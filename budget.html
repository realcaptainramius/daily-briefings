NDAA Status

FY27 National Defense Authorization Act: House/Senate progress, conference, and IT/cyber/space provisions.

Nov 9–13First lame-duck week passes with no Senate NDAA floor action; Democrats will hold both chambers in January

The Senate used its first floor vote of the lame duck (73-25 on the evening of Nov. 9) to begin debate on a bipartisan permitting/energy-affordability bill, not the NDAA. Some trackers had attributed that vote to the defense bill, but multiple reports confirm it was the permitting bill. No new cloture attempt on S. 4784 was found as of this run (Nov. 16). The Senate bill is still stuck after its July 14 cloture vote failed 50-46, while the House-passed H.R. 8800 (216-212, July 22) is the only chamber-passed text. The practical path now appears to be a compromise text negotiated by the four Armed Services leaders (Wicker, Reed, Rogers, Smith). One post-election outlook says Wicker has signaled the Senate will skip its own floor vote and negotiate off the House bill; that comes from a single source and is unconfirmed. A Nov. 10 letter from Sen. Van Hollen and colleagues to leadership and the Big Four, urging conferees to drop three U.S.-Israel cooperation provisions, indicates preconference talks are active. Also on Nov. 10, the House voted 211-204 to refer Rep. Massie's Hegseth impeachment resolution to committee, which kills it for this Congress, though Democratic leaders say the fight will return in 2027. The bigger change is the midterm result: Democrats won control of both the House and Senate. That puts Adam Smith in line to chair HASC and gives Democrats leverage to wait for January if they dislike a lame-duck deal. The NDAA's IT/cyber/space provisions, including the SDA dissolution language, are still proposed text, not law.

Appropriations & Continuing Resolution

Defense appropriations bill status and government-funding/shutdown risk.

Nov 2026Dec. 11 deadline nears as Democrats weigh Iran-war limits on the next CR; full-year Pentagon CR risk rises

Fiscal 2027 is still running on the stopgap (H.R. 6500) that holds funding at FY26 levels through December 11, which leaves the Pentagon at about $858B a year. None of the 12 FY27 bills has been enacted. The House defense bill ($1.07T, cleared full committee June 24) still hasn't had a floor vote, and Senate appropriators still haven't marked up theirs. With a Thanksgiving recess the week of Nov. 23 cutting into floor time, Air & Space Forces reports that another short-term CR is all but certain. The open questions are how long it runs and what's attached. A group of Senate Democrats (joined by Sanders) is pressing for any CR to restrict Iran-war spending, by barring supplemental war money, limiting fund transfers, or barring ground troops. Jeffries hasn't ruled out a shutdown over it, while Murray and DeLauro have favored a short extension without committing on war language. Holland & Knight (Nov. 13) says the risk of a full-year CR for the Pentagon, which first happened in FY25, has risen since the midterms. That would mean no new starts and no production increases for all of FY27 unless Congress grants anomalies. For IT/cyber/SATCOM pipelines, that's the scenario to plan for.

No changeStill no reported Senate Defense markup, nine weeks after McConnell's return

Sen. McConnell, chairman of the Senate Appropriations Defense Subcommittee, returned on September 14 after a three-month absence. No Senate Defense markup has been announced or reported since then. With less than four weeks until December 11 and a recess week in between, a regular-order Senate bill before the deadline now looks out of reach. Any full-year defense number would come through a leadership-negotiated package or wait for the new Democratic majorities in January.

Budget Priorities & Program Impacts

Reprogramming actions, budget requests, and funding moves affecting IT/cyber/space programs.

Nov 2026The $1.5T FY27 defense request is widely called dead after the midterms; Smith signals about $1T

Analysts quoted by Air & Space Forces, Bloomberg and Fortune (Nov. 15) say the White House's $1.5T plan is effectively dead now that Democrats have won both chambers. The plan pairs $1.15T in base discretionary funding with $350B the administration wanted through reconciliation. Rep. Adam Smith, the likely next HASC chairman, has said defense spending should not exceed about $1T. Bloomberg reports Democrats are likely to approve only about $1T. The Fiscal Times (Nov. 12) reports Trump is renewing his push for the full package, and some Republicans want reconciliation passed before they lose control in January. A reported Pentagon fallback of about $1.1T comes from unnamed sources and is unconfirmed. Separately, the $67B Pentagon share of the administration's $87.6B Iran-war supplemental request is still pending. Wicker supports it, DeLauro opposes it, and it would likely need Senate Democratic votes. For IT/cyber/space line items, a topline near $1T rather than $1.15T would squeeze FY27 growth programs first. Golden Dome's ~$17B reconciliation-dependent share is the most exposed, and no new Golden Dome funding action was found this week.

Fortune · Bloomberg via Yahoo · Air & Space Forces · Fiscal Times · Washington Examiner (supplemental) · House Approps Democrats
Oct–Nov 2026150+ FY27 new starts remain frozen, and a full-year CR would extend the freeze

Breaking Defense counts more than 150 new-start line items across the FY27 procurement and RDT&E budget documents that can't begin under the CR, including new drones, Golden Fleet, hypersonics and Golden Dome. The CR also bars production-rate increases. Government Executive (November) adds that a long-term CR would keep the Army from starting production on the MV-75 and XM30. Those are examples of how the freeze would last all year if the Pentagon ends up on a full-year CR. The Pentagon's ~$1.5B reprogramming request (reported by Breaking Defense in September), which includes $600M for a top-secret AI compute center on JWICS, still has no publicly reported final disposition.

Contract Vehicles & Procurement Policy

Major IT/cyber/SATCOM contract-vehicle timelines and the budget/policy context behind them.

No changeMegatron Industry Day still unscheduled, and the draft RFP now waits on it

As of this run (Nov. 16), there is still no new date for the postponed virtual Industry Day on the Air Force's "Megatron" DAF Battle Network IDIQ (Notice ID FA861227RB001). It was originally set for Oct. 7 and postponed over secure-platform problems and heavy registration volume. The SAM.gov notice now says draft solicitation Sections L and M will be posted only after the Industry Day happens, which replaces the earlier late-Q4 FY26 draft-RFP target. Award was last targeted for Q3 FY27 (~summer 2027) and could slip further. The ceiling is commonly cited at $4.5B+, but the Air Force hasn't confirmed a number.

No changeJWCC UCM Core: six weeks after the Oct. 6 due date, still no bidder count

Offers on DISA's $21.6B "UCM Core" hyperscaler cloud IDIQ (5-year base + 5-year option) were due October 6. No reporting on the number of offerors or their identities was found as of Nov. 16. The companion "UCM Premier" vehicle, for non-hyperscale and small-business/commercial-innovator cloud offerings, is still expected to get a final solicitation in Q1 CY2027 with awards in Q3 2027. That's the one to watch if you're not AWS, Google, Microsoft or Oracle.

No changeCMMC Task Force report still unpublished; Phase 2 stays suspended past its original Nov. 10 start

The CMMC Reform Task Force delivered its findings to the DoW CIO around September 11, and the report still hasn't been made public as of this run (Nov. 16). The most recent coverage found is from mid-October and says the CIO has committed to releasing it but hasn't set a date. November 10, the original start date for Phase 2 third-party assessments, has now passed with the requirement still suspended under Rev. 3 of DARS Class Deviation 2026-O0025 (issued Sept. 3). DFARS 252.204-7012, NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2, SPRS scoring and the annual affirmation all remain in force.
