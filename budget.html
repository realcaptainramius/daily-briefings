NDAA Status

FY27 National Defense Authorization Act: House/Senate progress, conference, and IT/cyber/space provisions.

No changeSenate still hasn't moved — standoff now in its tenth week

Nothing new to report on the core stalemate: the House passed its FY27 NDAA (H.R. 8800, $1.15T) 216-212 back in July, largely on party lines. The Senate's own version (S. 4784) has been stuck since a July 14 cloture vote failed 50-46, with Democrats blocking debate over the still-unauthorized Iran conflict and the defense topline. No conference committee has been formally seated — if this follows the FY26 precedent, HASC and SASC leaders will negotiate a bicameral text informally once the Senate finally passes something, rather than through a traditional conference. Until the Senate acts, the bill's IT/cyber/space provisions below are still just proposed text, not law.

Breaking Defense · Breaking Defense
Sep 15SDA's own director confirms the agency doesn't know if it survives conference

Both chambers' NDAA drafts carry language repealing the statute that created the Space Development Agency, folding its authorities into the Space Force's new portfolio acquisition executive (PAE) structure. Speaking at AFA's Air, Space and Cyber Conference, SDA Director Gurpartap Sandhoo said the agency's fast-decision authorities (special hiring, original classification, head-of-contracting) are what's actually at stake — "if we keep those, then that doesn't change anything... but if you lose some of those, there'll be some challenges." He put the final call outside his control: "If that's what the NDAA is, that's what the NDAA is." This is the clearest on-record acknowledgment yet that a name change is the optimistic outcome and a real authorities cut is the pessimistic one — worth tracking for anyone selling into proliferated-LEO comms and data-transport work, since it determines who holds the pen on those buys next year.

Appropriations & Continuing Resolution

Defense appropriations bill status and government-funding/shutdown risk.

No changeCR through Dec. 11 remains the operative funding mechanism

Still governed by the stopgap (H.R. 6500) Trump signed September 2, funding the government at FY26 levels through December 11. On the defense bill specifically: the House Appropriations Defense Subcommittee's FY27 bill cleared full committee back on June 24 (34-27) but still hasn't had a floor vote, and Senate appropriators haven't marked up their version at all. Nothing moved on either front this week. Bottom line unchanged from last week: no new-start IT/cyber/SATCOM money is fully appropriated, and won't be before December at the earliest — a long CR like this one typically freezes new starts and caps existing programs at prior-year funding profiles, which matters if your pipeline depends on a program getting its first tranche of FY27 dollars.

CRFB Appropriations Watch
Sep 14McConnell back in the Senate — the log-jam on Senate Defense markup may finally break

Sen. Mitch McConnell, chairman of the Senate Appropriations Defense Subcommittee, returned to the Capitol on September 14 after a three-month absence following a June fall and subsequent pneumonia. His absence has been the specific, named reason Senate appropriators haven't marked up a Defense bill all year — without him, Chair Collins couldn't get the votes to move it even on a partisan basis. His return doesn't guarantee a markup gets scheduled soon (there's no Defense markup on the committee's calendar as of this writing, and questions about his capacity persist), but it's the first structural change to the "why is Senate Defense stuck" story in months and worth watching closely over the next few weeks.

Contract Vehicles & Procurement Policy

Major IT/cyber/SATCOM contract-vehicle timelines and the budget/policy context behind them.

Sep 16Air Force stands up "Megatron" — a new multi-award IDIQ for Battle Network/C2 work

The Air Force's C3BM portfolio acquisition executive, Brig. Gen. Joshua Williams, confirmed the service is building a new multi-award IDIQ called Megatron to replace the XA and ABMS vehicles (both closed to new vendors) for DAF Battle Network work — the system-of-systems architecture connecting roughly 50 programs of record and serving as the Air/Space Force's contribution to CJADC2. It's a hybrid supply-and-services vehicle meant to span multiple appropriations and acquisition pathways (commercial solutions, continuous software delivery, software pathways all in scope). Ceiling and period of performance aren't finalized — some trade press has floated $4.5B+, but Williams declined to confirm a number — with a special notice and draft SOW out since August and an RFP expected this month. This is squarely in-lane for network/C2/tactical-comms integrators selling into USAF; worth tracking the RFP release closely.

The companion "UCM Premier" vehicle for non-hyperscale and small-business/commercial-innovator cloud offerings still isn't expected to get a final solicitation until Q1 CY2027, with awards in Q3 2027 — that's the one to watch if you're not AWS/Google/Microsoft/Oracle.

Nextgov/FCW
Sep 9CMMC reform task force findings due late Sept/early Oct — Phase 2 still suspended

The Pentagon is still sorting through industry feedback on its CMMC Reform Task Force RFI (comments closed mid-August); a public report and recommendations are expected between late September and early October. Phase 2 third-party assessment requirements — originally set to kick in November 10 — remain suspended pending that review. This isn't a contract vehicle, but it's a live procurement-policy question that directly affects compliance cost and timeline for any IT/cyber contractor handling CUI, so it belongs on this tracker rather than the daily program briefing. Worth a close read once the task force report lands.
