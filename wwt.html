USAF/USSF Leadership Watch

Public statements and appearances from Air Force and Space Force leadership — reactive coverage, sourced after the fact.

Sept 3Gen. Douglas Schiess sworn in as new Chief of Space Operations

Schiess succeeded Gen. Chance Saltzman as CSO on Sept 3 — only the third Chief of Space Operations in the Space Force's history. Saltzman grew the service from roughly 8,000 to 10,000 uniformed Guardians over his four-year tenure. This is a leadership transition worth tracking closely: a new CSO typically resets messaging priorities in his first 6–12 months, and his early public statements (see next item) are the clearest early signal of where USSF emphasis is headed.

Air & Space Forces Magazine · SpacePolicyOnline
Sept 15Schiess's first major address as CSO: Space Force was decisive in Operation Epic Fury

At AFA's Air, Space & Cyber Conference, Schiess said Guardians applied electronic-warfare effects to suppress enemy air defenses and communications during Operation Epic Fury (the campaign against Iran), and that Space Force capabilities were part of the calculus on whether the joint campaign could proceed at all: "Without the Space Force's capabilities, the Joint Force could not — and would not — move forward." Notable for anyone selling EW-adjacent, comms, or space-enabled C2 capabilities — this is the new CSO explicitly tying Space Force relevance to real combat operations rather than abstract deterrence.

Air & Space Forces Magazine
Sept 14CMSSF Bentivegna keynote: "The Cyber Thread of Space Power"

Chief Master Sergeant of the Space Force John Bentivegna's AFA keynote, titled "The Cyber Thread of Space Power: Forging the Guardians Who Secure Our Decision Advantage," framed cyber defense as core to Guardian identity and to protecting the data/decision pipeline space systems feed into. Directly relevant to anyone pitching cyber, identity, or data-security capabilities into USSF — this is the senior enlisted leader explicitly naming "decision advantage" security as a workforce and mission priority.

ThemeAI moving from pilot to production ↔ DoD's "pace-setting" AI mandate

AI/ML opsWWT's AI Proving Ground content is currently focused on AI-as-recurring-opex, i.e. getting past pilots into real production workloads. That lines up with the Department of War's January 2026 AI Strategy, which explicitly calls for measurable "pace-setting projects" rather than more experimentation, and with the Pentagon CIO's public line (from your DOW briefing) that the department "can't keep throwing people at" its tech gaps and needs to lean on automation instead. Both the market conversation and the customer conversation have moved past "should we use AI" to "how fast can we operationalize it."
