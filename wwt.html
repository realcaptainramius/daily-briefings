USAF/USSF Leadership Watch

Public statements and appearances from Air Force and Space Force leadership — reactive coverage, sourced after the fact.

Sept 15 (reported Sept 18)Schiess names 3 lines of effort as CSO: Guardian Ethos, Arm & Grow, Integrate Guardian Power

In a keynote at AFA's Air, Space & Cyber Conference, CSO Gen. Douglas Schiess laid out his formal priorities 12 days into the job: forging a "Guardian ethos," arming/growing the force, and integrating Guardian power into joint operations rather than a supporting role. On acquisition, he pointed to the VICTUS HAZE mission (Space Systems Command + industry responding to an on-orbit threat within 17 hours) as the model for the speed he wants across the portfolio. This is a fuller readout of the same AFA keynote flagged last week (Epic Fury remarks) — the new detail is the explicit 3-line framework that will likely structure USSF messaging through Spacepower in December.

ExecutiveGov
Sept 15 (reported Sept 18)Space Force flags FY27 CR drag on programs; creates new Technology Portfolio Executive for industry prototyping

Speaking alongside Schiess, new space acquisition chief Erich Hernandez-Baquero said the FY2027 continuing resolution (funding paused at 2026 levels through Dec. 11, no new starts permitted) will slow roughly 10% of Space Force programs even though "the threat is not slowing down." More relevant for industry: he's standing up a new Technology Portfolio Executive role — a single owner, reporting directly to him, dedicated full-time to identifying promising tech and running prototyping efforts to mature it to a hand-off point for program acquisition executives. The role exists on paper but is not yet filled. Worth tracking as a new formal on-ramp for vendor prototype engagement once someone is named to it.

Nextgov/FCW
Sept 14–15SECAF Meink & CSAF Wilsbach: Air Force targeting a "radically different" force by 2032

Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink and Chief of Staff Gen. Ken Wilsbach used their AFA keynotes to describe a service confronting vulnerable bases and a need to generate far more combat power without simply buying more expensive aircraft — leaning instead on autonomous aircraft and lower-cost weapons by 2032. Framed more around force structure and autonomy than IT/data specifically, but the "do more without linearly more expensive platforms" framing is the same logic driving software-defined and data-centric arguments elsewhere in the department.
