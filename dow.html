Nov 23–25Pentagon makes Air Force’s AI-aided ACME tool its classification-management system, with the Air Force secretary as executive agent

On Nov 23 the Pentagon announced Deputy Secretary Steve Feinberg’s October decision (a memo dated Oct 5) to make the Automated Classification Management Environment (ACME), a suite of AI-aided tools built by the Air Force and formerly known as Battering Ram, the department’s system for classification and declassification management, with Air Force Secretary Troy Meink as DoW executive agent. The memo calls for a limited automated classification capability within 180 days and, if the pilot meets its goals, for ACME to become the department’s “single, digital authoritative reference” for original classification decisions. Air & Space Forces Magazine (Nov 25) notes that neither the release nor earlier SAM.gov notices say which AI tools or models will be used or give a full-fielding timeline; former Air Force CIO Gen. William Bender said the work needs special precautions, including use of the department’s zero-trust architecture. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 24SpaceX launches classified USSF-266 mission for the Space Force from Vandenberg

A Falcon 9 launched the classified USSF-266 mission from Vandenberg’s SLC-4E at about 8:08 a.m. Pacific on Nov 24, after earlier listed dates of Nov 16–23; the booster landed on a droneship. The payload and orbit have not been officially disclosed. Launch-tracking sites speculate it carries Starshield-based or SB-AMTI satellites, but neither the Space Force nor SpaceX has confirmed that, and nothing links it to the Space Data Network Backbone. Outlets disagree on which NSSL contract it flew under and how to count the month’s national security launches. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 20–22USSF-317 launches; Space Force says first Space Data Network Backbone satellites are in orbit after three launches in two weeks

A Falcon 9 launched the classified USSF-317 mission from Vandenberg’s SLC-4E at 8:20 p.m. Pacific on Friday, Nov 20 (04:20 UTC Nov 21), after slips from Nov 18 and 19; the booster landed on a droneship. Air & Space Forces Magazine reports the Space Force said “three successful launches in two weeks” put the first Space Data Network Backbone satellites in orbit, and SpaceNews (Nov 22) reports the service is speeding deployment and seeking more industry partners to connect the backbone to other military constellations. Coverage does not confirm which three launches were meant, so whether USSF-317 carried SDN Backbone satellites remains unconfirmed; the payload is classified. Satellite counts and orbits are undisclosed. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 24–25Northwood Space says its Portal phased array completed two-way GEO contacts for a Space Force prototype, clearing the way to negotiate production

Northwood Space announced on Nov 24 that its Portal phased-array ground antenna held full-duplex contacts with a U.S. government satellite in geosynchronous orbit, the final milestone of a prototype contract with Space Systems Command through the Portfolio Acquisition Executive for Battle Management Command, Control, Communications and Space Intelligence (BMC3I). The contacts were scheduled through the Space Force’s Joint Antenna Marketplace. The company says completing the milestone lets program offices negotiate production agreements without a new contract; no production award has been announced. Some reports say the contacts actually took place in September (one cites Sept. 17) from a Ventura, California, site built in seven days, so only the announcement falls in this window. Defense Daily and Via Satellite covered it on Nov 25. Based on the company’s release as relayed in search summaries; the trade articles themselves were not seen.

Nov 24–25NYT: CIA/ODNI open-source intelligence system breached; AI hacking tool ARTEX also reportedly used in low-impact intrusion at DoD’s Cyber Defense Command; Microsoft names six groups using ARTEX

The New York Times reported on Nov 24, citing unnamed sources, that attackers breached an unclassified open-source intelligence analysis system used by the CIA and ODNI, with NGA also affected; sources say no classified systems or data were touched, and ODNI said critical intelligence capabilities were not disrupted. The Times’ sources say the attackers used ARTEX, an open-source AI-driven penetration-testing framework the FBI warned about on Nov 13. Separately, Nextgov/FCW reports, citing two people familiar with the matter, that ARTEX was used in a breach at the Defense Department’s Cyber Defense Command that one called low-impact. The NYT’s sources said early signs pointed to China, which denies such claims. On Nov 25 Microsoft published research naming six groups it tracks using ARTEX, among them China-linked Salt Typhoon and Crystal Typhoon, North Korea-linked Onyx Sleet and Pearl Sleet, and Storm-0940 and Storm-3358; Microsoft did not attribute the intelligence-system breach to any of them. Attribution of both intrusions is unconfirmed, no government has publicly attributed them, and they have not been linked to the same actor. Seen via search summaries only; Microsoft’s report was seen only through secondary coverage.

Nov 23–25White House asks Congress for defense “anomalies” in the next stopgap, including $5B for Golden Dome and faster spending to rebuild the MQ-9 fleet

Politico reported late on Nov 23 that the White House sent Capitol Hill a list of exceptions (“anomalies”) for the next continuing resolution; Punchbowl says it assumes a stopgap through March 31. Inside Defense puts the defense-related requests at about $31 billion across nearly two dozen programs, while some outlets cite about $22 billion. The list reportedly includes more than $21 billion for Columbia- and Virginia-class submarines, shipbuilding authority including the Trump-class battleship, and $5 billion for Golden Dome, which the White House says otherwise “will be unable to continue production”; it says more Department of War anomalies are forthcoming. Air & Space Forces Magazine (Nov 25) reports the list also seeks authority for the Air Force to spend faster than standard CR rules allow to rebuild its MQ-9 Reaper fleet after dozens were lost in the Iran war. This is a request; Congress is not required to include it and has not acted on it. Seen via search summaries only.

National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross has been removed, according to a White House official and people familiar with the matter, after a week of major outages across aviation, government and commercial IT; some outlets describe it as a resignation. Nov 17–20NSANet outage, tied to patching, resolved after nearly four days; officials tell House panel the wave of outages was not malicious

CNN first reported on Tuesday, Nov 17, that NSANet, the NSA’s classified intranet for secret and top-secret internal communications, went down early Tuesday and stayed down into the afternoon, following other interruptions over the past month. The NSA said the network suffered temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance and that mission-critical capabilities stayed up. A source told the WSJ a system patch was under way beforehand, and sources said there was no evidence of a cyberattack. Reports say staff arrivals were staggered and that the campus was later closed to most employees until further notice. DNI Jay Clayton linked the outage to patching after a PHP vulnerability was found, saying some systems did not come back up as expected; some outlets report the network has been restored, citing unnamed sources, but the NSA has not confirmed it. At a House Homeland Security Committee hearing on Nov 18, Clayton, FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said they had seen no sign of malicious or state-sponsored activity behind the recent run of outages, pointing instead to faster patching and aging infrastructure. The Record reports disruptions continued into a third day on Nov 19 with the Fort Meade campus still closed to most non-essential staff. CNN reported on Friday, Nov 20, citing a source and an employee voicemail recording, that the outage had been resolved, with staff returning on staggered arrivals to avoid overloading logons; the NSA itself has not publicly confirmed restoration. NBC News places the outage in a week of unrelated major outages, including the FAA’s, that stoked sabotage fears, though researchers point to brittle systems instead. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 12–25Lame-duck budget fight: $350B defense reconciliation push stalls; Senate GOP open to stopgap with Iran-war limits as Dec 11 CR expiry nears

The Pentagon is seeking a reconciliation package of at least $350 billion on top of its $1.15 trillion request, while the current continuing resolution holds DoD at FY26 levels through Dec 11. The CISA 2015 threat-sharing law also lapses Dec 11 unless extended, which the House NDAA would do through 2035. On Nov 12 the Washington Times quoted outgoing SASC Chairman Roger Wicker warning that Golden Dome may not be fully funded now that Democrats control both chambers, putting its reconciliation money in doubt. Deputy Secretary Steve Feinberg was on Capitol Hill on Wednesday, Nov 18, and The Hill reports he discussed the $350 billion reconciliation request at a Senate Republican lunch; House Armed Services Chairman Mike Rogers said the figure “is not gonna happen,” and Defense One reports Republicans left without consensus. On Monday, Nov 16, Senate Republican appropriators unveiled a nearly $1.1 trillion FY27 defense appropriations bill that folds about $69 billion of the White House’s reconciliation priorities into regular discretionary funding. Breaking Defense reports it gives Golden Dome $9.4 billion, well short of the roughly $17 billion sought (almost all through reconciliation) but enough to keep the program going, plus $3 billion for the Defense Autonomous Warfare Group. The bill still has to pass the Senate and be reconciled with the House’s roughly $1.07 trillion version; some coverage puts the Senate total at $1.072 trillion instead. The bill also limits sole-source space awards. The administration is reportedly seeking about $17.1 billion for Golden Dome through reconciliation (a figure seen only in a low-tier outlet). The Hill reports Sen. Mike Rounds warned a lame-duck reconciliation push would raise shutdown risk, and Senate Majority Leader Thune has reportedly discouraged it privately. CyberScoop reports Sen. Rand Paul has vowed to block a CISA 2015 reauthorization without limits on CISA’s counter-disinformation work. An Inside Defense headline the same day says the likely next House defense appropriations chair wants the Pentagon to stop relying on reconciliation. Punchbowl reported Nov 17 that end-stage NDAA negotiations were starting that week; no compromise text had been released as of Nov 19. USNI News (Nov 18) reports the Senate bill provides $41.6 billion for Navy shipbuilding, $18.5 billion below the request. Holland & Knight says the risk of a year-long CR for the Pentagon has risen, and as of Nov 18 leaders had not agreed how to extend funding past Dec 11, with another stopgap into early 2027 under discussion. On Nov 19 Axios reported Republicans were losing hope in the party-line reconciliation push, with Senate Republicans pausing it to try a bipartisan route first. DefenseScoop (Nov 19) quotes Golden Dome deputy director Marcia Holmes saying the CR is not slowing the program because its funds are fully obligated and the 2028 deliverable stands. A Kaine staffer said Nov 17 that conferenced NDAA text is a “couple weeks” away. The Washington Times (Nov 18) reports Thune and Budget Chairman Ron Johnson said a budget resolution was unlikely to reach the floor that week but reconciliation is not off the table, after Thune had said one would be needed by Thanksgiving to unlock the $350 billion. Per a secondary summary of Politico Pro, HASC Chairman Rogers said Nov 19 the House could vote on a compromise NDAA as soon as lawmakers return from Thanksgiving, while Ranking Member Adam Smith said disputes remain, including right-to-repair and limits on defense-industry stock buybacks. Punchbowl reports the administration is blaming Thune for the all-but-certain collapse of the party-line plan, with a senior official saying “our adversaries are not waiting” for Senate Republicans. Congress is out for Thanksgiving and returns the week of Nov 30, per The Fiscal Times (Nov 23). The Hill reports senators are eyeing a deal that would fund the government through mid-April and carry the NDAA, with the main fight over Democratic language, being drafted with Sen. Tim Kaine, that would bar funding for the Iran war with exceptions for defensive operations; Sen. Susan Collins said she is “looking at language that might be able to do that,” Sen. John Kennedy put shutdown odds at better than 50–50, and Sen. Tammy Duckworth said she will oppose any deal that does not cut off war funding. Trump has threatened vetoes, so any Iran restriction would need veto-proof margins. On Nov 24, Majority Leader Thune was reported as open to “anything that keeps the government funded” and meets military needs while calling a ban on war funding in a stopgap “short-sighted”; Sen. Collins is reportedly exploring a deal funding munitions restocking but not offensive Iran operations without authorization. The Washington Times reported on Nov 25 that Senate Republicans are open to a deal with Democrats that would fund munitions restocking while restricting money for offensive Iran operations without congressional authorization; Sen. John Hoeven said there is “room for negotiation” but any deal must be acceptable to the administration and could not undercut the war effort, and reports say the restriction would last only as long as the stopgap. Democrats need at least seven Republican votes to reach 60 on a stopgap while Republicans hold 53 seats until Jan 3. The White House also sent a list of stopgap “anomalies” (see separate item). These are negotiating positions, not agreed text; no conference NDAA text or new stopgap had been released as of Nov 26. Analyst expectations are forecasts, not decisions.
