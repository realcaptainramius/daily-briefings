Nov 16–17Senate defense spending bill would bar sole-source awards over $100M for the Space Data Network and SB-AMTI

SatNews reports that the Senate Appropriations Committee’s FY27 defense bill, released Monday, Nov 16, would prohibit sole-source or noncompetitive contracts above $100 million for two satellite programs: the Space Data Network and the Space-Based Airborne Moving Target Indicator (SB-AMTI). The provision still has to survive the Senate and negotiations with the House. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 15–19Space Force adds up to $985M to NSSL Phase 3 Lane 2 launch contracts as Vulcan failure deepens launch-capacity worries

The Nov 16 DoW contracts list includes modifications from Space Systems Command’s Space Access office to SpaceX, United Launch Services and Blue Origin with a combined maximum ceiling of $985 million for National Security Space Launch Phase 3, Lane 2 launch services, with work running through April 2033. The figure is a ceiling, not booked revenue, and Defence Blog says the announcement does not give the split among the three companies. The award came a day after ULA’s Vulcan failed about 10 minutes into its Nov 15 return-to-flight launch, losing 40 Amazon Leo satellites. Vulcan had been grounded for national security missions since a February solid-rocket-booster anomaly on USSF-87. Coverage citing Reuters says the Pentagon had been waiting on this flight before clearing Vulcan to fly them again. Vulcan is contracted for roughly half of upcoming national security launches, and the Space Force moved six of them to SpaceX in October. Defense Daily (Nov 16) says the failure sets back the Space Force’s effort to rely on more providers than SpaceX. Breaking Defense says a lengthy investigation could keep Vulcan grounded for months. ULA said in a Nov 18 update that preliminary data point to an inadequate fuel supply from the vehicle to the first stage’s BE-4 engines, not to the engines’ own performance, without naming the component responsible; Blue Origin said it agrees, and the Northrop Grumman solid rocket boosters appear to have performed normally. The FAA is overseeing the ULA-led investigation. Air & Space Forces Magazine notes the failure could sideline two of the Space Force’s three NSSL providers, since New Glenn also flies on BE-4 engines and has not flown since May. No return-to-flight date has been set, and as of Nov 23 no Space Force statement on Vulcan’s national security missions or further reassignments, and no newer investigation update, was found. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 13–19Nationwide FAA ground stop preliminarily traced to router work by an L3Harris contractor

A failure in the FAA’s communications network on Friday, Nov 13, caused a nationwide ground stop that lasted about four hours. Newsweek reports officials called it the largest since 9/11, and Saturday cancellations reportedly topped 6,000. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a preliminary investigation found an L3Harris contractor at a remote FAA site in El Dorado, Arkansas, was moving copper lines to fiber and replacing a router. That router began flooding the network with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, and a second router in Texas did the same. Investigators have not determined whether the cause was human error, installation or the equipment, and the router maker told the WSJ the devices worked as intended. L3Harris operates the network for the FAA and says it is supporting the investigation. Homeland Security Secretary Mullin said there was no indication of a cyberattack, though Fortune (Nov 15) says Duffy has not fully ruled one out. L3Harris says it had completed more than 70% of the copper-to-fiber conversion. Later reports say the outage hit 17 of the FAA’s 22 en-route centers. As a precaution, technicians disabled a broadcast function on about 60 similar routers, and L3Harris is testing the routers at its Florida headquarters. The FBI is also investigating, and L3Harris has not accepted responsibility. CNN reported on Nov 16 that the FAA had fielded years of warnings about its aging systems. On Wednesday, Nov 18, Duffy said L3Harris failed to identify and isolate the faulty router quickly enough and that the ground stop could have ended sooner, while saying investigators still have not determined whether the cause was a hardware defect or a network-management failure; FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said he takes “full responsibility,” and new installations of the router model have been paused. Per Nov 19 reports, the routers from El Dorado and Amarillo, Texas are being examined at an L3Harris lab in Florida, where engineers reproduced the overload on Nov 18, and the router maker says the device worked as the installer configured it. No official findings had been released, and no newer reporting was found, as of Nov 23. This is a civil FAA contract, not DoW, and was seen via search summaries only.

Nov 17–20NSANet outage, tied to patching, resolved after nearly four days; officials tell House panel the wave of outages was not malicious

CNN first reported on Tuesday, Nov 17, that NSANet, the NSA’s classified intranet for secret and top-secret internal communications, went down early Tuesday and stayed down into the afternoon, following other interruptions over the past month. The NSA said the network suffered temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance and that mission-critical capabilities stayed up. A source told the WSJ a system patch was under way beforehand, and sources said there was no evidence of a cyberattack. Reports say staff arrivals were staggered and that the campus was later closed to most employees until further notice. DNI Jay Clayton linked the outage to patching after a PHP vulnerability was found, saying some systems did not come back up as expected; some outlets report the network has been restored, citing unnamed sources, but the NSA has not confirmed it. At a House Homeland Security Committee hearing on Nov 18, Clayton, FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said they had seen no sign of malicious or state-sponsored activity behind the recent run of outages, pointing instead to faster patching and aging infrastructure. The Record reports disruptions continued into a third day on Nov 19 with the Fort Meade campus still closed to most non-essential staff. CNN reported on Friday, Nov 20, citing a source and an employee voicemail recording, that the outage had been resolved, with staff returning on staggered arrivals to avoid overloading logons; the NSA itself has not publicly confirmed restoration. NBC News places the outage in a week of unrelated major outages, including the FAA’s, that stoked sabotage fears, though researchers point to brittle systems instead. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 12–23Lame-duck budget fight: $350B defense reconciliation push stalls; senators weigh stopgap with Iran-war limits as Dec 11 CR expiry nears

The Pentagon is seeking a reconciliation package of at least $350 billion on top of its $1.15 trillion request, while the current continuing resolution holds DoD at FY26 levels through Dec 11. The CISA 2015 threat-sharing law also lapses Dec 11 unless extended, which the House NDAA would do through 2035. On Nov 12 the Washington Times quoted outgoing SASC Chairman Roger Wicker warning that Golden Dome may not be fully funded now that Democrats control both chambers, putting its reconciliation money in doubt. Deputy Secretary Steve Feinberg was on Capitol Hill on Wednesday, Nov 18, and The Hill reports he discussed the $350 billion reconciliation request at a Senate Republican lunch; House Armed Services Chairman Mike Rogers said the figure “is not gonna happen,” and Defense One reports Republicans left without consensus. On Monday, Nov 16, Senate Republican appropriators unveiled a nearly $1.1 trillion FY27 defense appropriations bill that folds about $69 billion of the White House’s reconciliation priorities into regular discretionary funding. Breaking Defense reports it gives Golden Dome $9.4 billion, well short of the roughly $17 billion sought (almost all through reconciliation) but enough to keep the program going, plus $3 billion for the Defense Autonomous Warfare Group. The bill still has to pass the Senate and be reconciled with the House’s roughly $1.07 trillion version; some coverage puts the Senate total at $1.072 trillion instead. The bill also limits sole-source space awards (see the Air Force & Space Force section). The administration is reportedly seeking about $17.1 billion for Golden Dome through reconciliation (a figure seen only in a low-tier outlet). The Hill reports Sen. Mike Rounds warned a lame-duck reconciliation push would raise shutdown risk, and Senate Majority Leader Thune has reportedly discouraged it privately. CyberScoop reports Sen. Rand Paul has vowed to block a CISA 2015 reauthorization without limits on CISA’s counter-disinformation work. An Inside Defense headline the same day says the likely next House defense appropriations chair wants the Pentagon to stop relying on reconciliation. Punchbowl reported Nov 17 that end-stage NDAA negotiations were starting that week; no compromise text had been released as of Nov 19. USNI News (Nov 18) reports the Senate bill provides $41.6 billion for Navy shipbuilding, $18.5 billion below the request. Holland & Knight says the risk of a year-long CR for the Pentagon has risen, and as of Nov 18 leaders had not agreed how to extend funding past Dec 11, with another stopgap into early 2027 under discussion. On Nov 19 Axios reported Republicans were losing hope in the party-line reconciliation push, with Senate Republicans pausing it to try a bipartisan route first. DefenseScoop (Nov 19) quotes Golden Dome deputy director Marcia Holmes saying the CR is not slowing the program because its funds are fully obligated and the 2028 deliverable stands. A Kaine staffer said Nov 17 that conferenced NDAA text is a “couple weeks” away. The Washington Times (Nov 18) reports Thune and Budget Chairman Ron Johnson said a budget resolution was unlikely to reach the floor that week but reconciliation is not off the table, after Thune had said one would be needed by Thanksgiving to unlock the $350 billion. Per a secondary summary of Politico Pro, HASC Chairman Rogers said Nov 19 the House could vote on a compromise NDAA as soon as lawmakers return from Thanksgiving, while Ranking Member Adam Smith said disputes remain, including right-to-repair and limits on defense-industry stock buybacks. Punchbowl reports the administration is blaming Thune for the all-but-certain collapse of the party-line plan, with a senior official saying “our adversaries are not waiting” for Senate Republicans. Congress is out for Thanksgiving and returns the week of Nov 30, per The Fiscal Times (Nov 23). The Hill reports senators are eyeing a deal that would fund the government through mid-April and carry the NDAA, with the main fight over Democratic language, being drafted with Sen. Tim Kaine, that would bar funding for the Iran war with exceptions for defensive operations; Sen. Susan Collins said she is “looking at language that might be able to do that,” Sen. John Kennedy put shutdown odds at better than 50–50, and Sen. Tammy Duckworth said she will oppose any deal that does not cut off war funding. Trump has threatened vetoes, so any Iran restriction would need veto-proof margins. These are negotiating positions, not agreed text; no conference NDAA text or new stopgap had been released as of Nov 24. Analyst expectations are forecasts, not decisions.
