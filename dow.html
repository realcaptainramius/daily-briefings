Nov 11–13Space Force picks Anduril, Booz Allen and Peerless to prototype cloud C2 for its electromagnetic warfare systems

Space Systems Command and Combat Forces Command awarded Anduril Industries, Booz Allen Hamilton and Peerless Technologies Phase I prototype agreements, about $10 million in total, through the Space Enterprise Consortium OTA. The Space Electromagnetic Warfare Operating Location (SEWOL) effort is meant to replace the separate legacy tools that space EW (jamming) operators use with a single cloud-native command-and-control application and a common operating picture. Each vendor delivers a minimum viable product and an SDK over a roughly three-month phase within a reported $60 million prototype program. Aviation Week reports a down-select to one vendor is planned for early next year. The SSC release date was not confirmed (one summary says Nov 11); SpaceNews and Aviation Week reported the awards Nov 13. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 12Space Force and UT Austin launch BMC3I TAP Lab Texas for early-stage battle-management software

Space Systems Command and the University of Texas at Austin announced BMC3I TAP Lab Texas, an Austin site of the Space Force’s battle management, command, control, communications and space intelligence (BMC3I) software accelerator. It is funded by a $9.27 million Texas Space Commission grant and led by astrodynamicist Moriba Jah at UT’s Cockrell School. Coverage describes the Austin site as handling early-stage research and testing (roughly TRL 1–4) before tools move to the Colorado Springs BMC3I TAP Lab for operational adoption. Tech Times links the lab to the Kronos space battle-management program. One earlier post said the lab would open in Q1 2027, so the start of operations is unclear. The SSC release date was not seen; Tech Times dates its story Nov 12. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 11–12Department of the Air Force assigns AI to 15 mission areas, each with a senior owner; Falcon 9 launches classified USSF-627

Air & Space Forces Magazine and a SpaceForce.mil article report that the Department of the Air Force’s enterprise AI plan assigns AI to 15 mission areas, including intelligence, personnel, nuclear operations and space/C4ISR. Each area has a general officer or SES lead responsible for making authoritative data available and fielding tools faster, with progress reviewed at quarterly showcases. Chief Data and AI Officer Susan Davenport outlined the plan on Oct 27; the department declined to release the document, so the full list is unconfirmed, and the magazine article’s exact date was not seen (aggregators list it Nov 12). Separately, Space.com reports a Falcon 9 launched the classified USSF-627 mission to low Earth orbit from Vandenberg on Nov 11 Pacific time, after slips from Nov 9 and 10. It was SpaceX’s second classified Space Force launch in six days. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 5–10Space Force picks Viasat for Space Data Network Backbone mission operations center, up to $450M

Space Systems Command awarded Viasat an IDIQ contract with a $450 million ceiling, and a first task order of $115 million, to design, field, sustain and operate the mission operations center for the Space Data Network Backbone, part of the Space-Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio. The center is meant to let operators manage data traffic across multiple satellite constellations from one hub; SSC said the work is in partnership with Golden Dome for America, and reports say SpaceX is building the initial backbone. The $450 million is a ceiling, not booked revenue. Dates differ by source: an SSC release reportedly dated Nov 5, SpaceNews and GovConWire Nov 6, Air & Space Forces Magazine Nov 9, and Viasat’s release Nov 10. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 6–12Reported: Space Data Network laser links can’t interoperate, fix could cost ~$1B; SDA gives SpaceX a $9.9M laser-link study

Breaking Defense reported on Nov 6, citing unnamed industry and former government sources, that satellites in the laser-linked Space Data Network cannot readily exchange data with other military space systems, partly because SDA and SSC optical terminals were built to different standards. A fix under discussion would add 12–13 “Space Exchange Point” translator satellites, roughly $1 billion in new money; the Space Force has not confirmed the estimate. Separately, SDA awarded SpaceX a risk-reduction study contract on Nov 9 on integrating its “Plug and Play Laser” optical crosslink with missile-warning and tracking systems; $9.9 million was obligated at award, and options could bring it to $12.7 million. The award appeared in the Nov 9 contract announcements and was reported Nov 10–13. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 13–16Nationwide FAA ground stop preliminarily traced to router work by an L3Harris contractor

A failure in the FAA’s communications network on Friday, Nov 13, caused a nationwide ground stop that lasted about four hours. Newsweek reports officials called it the largest since 9/11, and Saturday cancellations reportedly topped 6,000. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a preliminary investigation found an L3Harris contractor at a remote FAA site in El Dorado, Arkansas, was moving copper lines to fiber and replacing a router. That router began flooding the network with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, and a second router in Texas did the same. Investigators have not determined whether the cause was human error, installation or the equipment, and the router maker told the WSJ the devices worked as intended. L3Harris operates the network for the FAA and says it is supporting the investigation. Homeland Security Secretary Mullin said there was no indication of a cyberattack, though Fortune (Nov 15) says Duffy has not fully ruled one out. L3Harris says it had completed more than 70% of the copper-to-fiber conversion. CNN reported on Nov 16 that the FAA had fielded years of warnings about its aging systems; no official findings had been released as of Nov 16. This is a civil FAA contract, not DoW, and was seen via search summaries only.

Nov 12Air Force picks Anduril to prototype a containerized launcher for its Counter-Air Missile Program

Anduril announced on Nov 12 that the Air Force awarded it a prototype contract for a common containerized launcher under the Counter-Air Missile Program, which supports the Ground Launched Counter Air effort to field affordable long-range missiles that engage aircraft from the ground. The initial period covers 10 months of preliminary design and preparation for integration testing with missiles from other manufacturers; the launcher is to provide common power, computing, cooling, communications and software interfaces. Contract value and type were not disclosed, and no Air Force release was seen. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 13Pentagon renames its Chief Digital and AI Office the “Super Intelligence Office”

The Department of War redesignated the CDAO as the Department of War Super Intelligence Office (SIO), citing President Trump’s September Executive Order 14434, which directs agencies to use “Super Intelligence” in official communications. The department says the office’s mission does not change. Cameron Stanley continues as head, now titled Chief Super Intelligence Officer, with Andrew Mapes as principal deputy. Existing programs, solicitations and partnerships continue. A new website is at SI.mil, and the change will roll out across systems and component headquarters over the coming weeks. One outlet reports GenAI.mil is also being renamed, but the official release summary does not say so. Seen via search summaries of the release and coverage.

Nov 6–17Lame-duck budget fight: Senate GOP unveils ~$1.1T defense bill with $9.4B for Golden Dome; CR expiry nears Dec 11

At his Nov 10 briefing Hegseth called the lame-duck session critical for department funding and sought a reconciliation package of at least $350 billion on top of the $1.15 trillion request. The current continuing resolution holds DoD at FY26 levels through Dec 11; analysts quoted by Forecast International and NPR expect another CR into spring after Democrats won both chambers, and NPR reports the FY27 NDAA is stalled. The CISA 2015 threat-sharing law also lapses Dec 11 unless extended, which the House NDAA would do through 2035. On Nov 12 the Washington Times quoted outgoing SASC Chairman Roger Wicker warning that Golden Dome may not be fully funded now that Democrats control both chambers, putting its reconciliation money in doubt. Wicker said Deputy Secretary Steve Feinberg would meet lawmakers early the week of Nov 16; those meetings had not been reported as of Nov 17. On Monday, Nov 16, Senate Republican appropriators unveiled a nearly $1.1 trillion FY27 defense appropriations bill that folds about $69 billion of the White House’s reconciliation priorities into regular discretionary funding. Breaking Defense reports it gives Golden Dome $9.4 billion, well short of the roughly $17 billion sought (almost all through reconciliation) but enough to keep the program going, plus $3 billion for the Defense Autonomous Warfare Group. The bill still has to pass the Senate and be reconciled with the House version. The administration is reportedly seeking about $17.1 billion for Golden Dome through reconciliation (a figure seen only in a low-tier outlet). The Hill reports Sen. Mike Rounds warned a lame-duck reconciliation push would raise shutdown risk, and Senate Majority Leader Thune has reportedly discouraged it privately. CyberScoop reports Sen. Rand Paul has vowed to block a CISA 2015 reauthorization without limits on CISA’s counter-disinformation work. An Inside Defense headline the same day says the likely next House defense appropriations chair wants the Pentagon to stop relying on reconciliation. NDAA trackers say HASC leaders want a compromise text before Thanksgiving; none was found as of Nov 17. Analyst expectations are forecasts, not decisions; seen via search summaries only.
