Air Force & Space Force — IT/Cyber/SATCOM/Comms

Top priority: USAF/USSF programs touching network, cyber, C2/JADC2, cloud, or proliferated LEO comms.

Sep 15Budget impasse could stall 10% of Space Force programs, officials warn

Space Force Chief of Space Operations Gen. Douglas Schiess told AFA's Air, Space & Cyber conference that a continuing resolution for the coming fiscal year would stall roughly 10% of Space Force programs. Translation: if a program you're chasing lives in that 10%, budget uncertainty — not competition — may be your near-term obstacle. Worth asking your program-office contacts directly which efforts are on the stall list.

Air & Space Forces Magazine · Defense One
Sep 15–16SDA's Tranche 1 constellation nearing completion, two more launches planned this year

SDA Director Gurpartap Sandhoo said the agency aims to complete two more Tranche 1 launches in 2026 (one next month, one in December), with the full six-batch campaign wrapping by spring 2027. Only 63 of the planned 154 first-operational satellites are on orbit so far, slowed by supply-chain issues and this year's long government shutdown — a real signal on schedule risk if you're building around SDA's tracking/data-transport layers.

Air & Space Forces Magazine · National Defense Magazine
Sep 16Air Force: "defend the ecosystem," not just the base — cyber defense for off-base power and water

USAF leadership and industry experts at AFA's conference pushed for cyber defense to extend beyond base perimeters to the power and water infrastructure that supports them, including dedicated offsite microgrids as a hedge against a cyberattack-triggered blackout. Direct relevance if any part of your book touches base-adjacent network resilience or critical-infrastructure protection contracts.

Air & Space Forces Magazine
Sep 15Space Force lays out plan to add 10,000 Guardians, more than double the force in 5 years

USSF leaders detailed at the conference how they intend to more than double the service's size over five years, with a large share of that growth front-loaded into year one. More Guardians eventually means more billets to staff programs and more acquisition throughput — a slow-burn tailwind for anyone selling into USSF, though it says nothing about near-term budget (see the CR item above).
