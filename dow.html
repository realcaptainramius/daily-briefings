Top priority: USAF/USSF programs touching network, cyber, C2/JADC2, cloud, or proliferated LEO comms.

Nov 11–12Department of the Air Force assigns AI to 15 mission areas, each with a senior owner; Falcon 9 launches classified USSF-627

Air & Space Forces Magazine and a SpaceForce.mil article report that the Department of the Air Force’s enterprise AI plan assigns AI to 15 mission areas, including intelligence, personnel, nuclear operations and space/C4ISR. Each area has a general officer or SES lead responsible for making authoritative data available and fielding tools faster, with progress reviewed at quarterly showcases. Chief Data and AI Officer Susan Davenport outlined the plan on Oct 27; the department declined to release the document, so the full list is unconfirmed, and the magazine article’s exact date was not seen (aggregators list it Nov 12). Separately, Space.com reports a Falcon 9 launched the classified USSF-627 mission to low Earth orbit from Vandenberg on Nov 11 Pacific time, after slips from Nov 9 and 10. It was SpaceX’s second classified Space Force launch in six days. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 5–10Space Force picks Viasat for Space Data Network Backbone mission operations center, up to $450M

Space Systems Command awarded Viasat an IDIQ contract with a $450 million ceiling, and a first task order of $115 million, to design, field, sustain and operate the mission operations center for the Space Data Network Backbone, part of the Space-Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio. The center is meant to let operators manage data traffic across multiple satellite constellations from one hub; SSC said the work is in partnership with Golden Dome for America, and reports say SpaceX is building the initial backbone. The $450 million is a ceiling, not booked revenue. Dates differ by source: an SSC release reportedly dated Nov 5, SpaceNews and GovConWire Nov 6, Air & Space Forces Magazine Nov 9, and Viasat’s release Nov 10. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 6–12Reported: Space Data Network laser links can’t interoperate, fix could cost ~$1B; SDA gives SpaceX a ~$10M bridging study

Breaking Defense reported on Nov 6, citing unnamed industry and former government sources, that satellites in the laser-linked Space Data Network cannot readily exchange data with other military space systems, partly because SDA and SSC optical terminals were built to different standards. A fix under discussion would add 12–13 “Space Exchange Point” translator satellites, roughly $1 billion in new money; the Space Force has not confirmed the estimate. SpaceNews separately reports that SDA awarded SpaceX a study contract worth nearly $10 million on integrating SpaceX’s proprietary optical crosslinks with missile-warning and tracking architectures; the article is paywalled and its exact date (Nov 11 or 12) was not confirmed. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 10Air Force completes classified Force Design 2026; issues RFI for AI to run cislunar spacecraft

The Air Force says it has completed Force Design 2026, a classified plan with no releasable unclassified version, emphasizing command-and-control and information systems that tie aircraft, sensors and weapons together, plus autonomy and integration; Gen. Ken Wilsbach called it “a fundamental shift.” Separately, DefenseScoop reports an Air Force RFI seeking off-the-shelf AI software to let spacecraft operate autonomously beyond near-Earth orbit. Other DoD/DOW News

Brief items: leadership, budget/policy, broader domain news.

Nov 6–12Lame-duck budget fight: Hegseth seeks at least $350B in reconciliation; Golden Dome funding in doubt as CR expiry nears Dec 11

At his Nov 10 briefing Hegseth called the lame-duck session critical for department funding and sought a reconciliation package of at least $350 billion on top of the $1.15 trillion request. The current continuing resolution holds DoD at FY26 levels through Dec 11; analysts quoted by Forecast International and NPR expect another CR into spring after Democrats won both chambers, and NPR reports the FY27 NDAA is stalled. The CISA 2015 threat-sharing law also lapses Dec 11 unless extended, which the House NDAA would do through 2035. On Nov 12 the Washington Times quoted outgoing SASC Chairman Roger Wicker warning that Golden Dome may not be fully funded now that Democrats control both chambers, putting its reconciliation money in doubt. An Inside Defense headline the same day says the likely next House defense appropriations chair wants the Pentagon to stop relying on reconciliation. NDAA trackers say HASC leaders want a compromise text before Thanksgiving. Analyst expectations are forecasts, not decisions; seen via search summaries only.

Nov 9GAO: about 17,000 F-35 parts missing from the program’s tracking system

GAO report GAO-27-108713 says the F-35 program has about 26 million parts worth about $31 billion at more than 700 sites. About 17,000 components are not recorded in its ELMS tracking system; the value is reported as about $1.3 billion, with one figure of $2.5 billion. The Joint Program Office still cannot account for more than $85 million in parts lost in 2023. DoD agreed with GAO’s four recommendations. Seen via search summaries.
