Oct 2Space Force retires the Cold War-era Defense Support Program missile-warning constellation

The Space Force has decommissioned the Defense Support Program (DSP), more than 50 years after the first DSP satellite launched in 1970. The last two spacecraft took part in a U.S. Space Command exercise in early September and were then moved to a supersynchronous graveyard orbit. Mission Delta 4 carries on with the Space Based Infrared System (SBIRS) while preparing for the first Next-Gen OPIR satellite. Coverage appeared Oct 2–3; the exact decommissioning date was not stated in the summaries reviewed.

Oct 2Space Force lays groundwork to connect future satellite “enclaves” ahead of more launches

DefenseScoop reports the Space Force is preparing the next batch of LEO satellite launches while laying groundwork for a Space Data Network of military and commercial satellites from different vendors. In May the department awarded SpaceX $2.3 billion for an operational prototype of the SDN “backbone” by the end of next year, with other transition agreements to Lockheed, Northrop, Rocket Lab and Amazon LEO for Government for ground-to-space and space-to-space data transport (per the article’s search summary; full text not accessible).

Oct 5–6SDA’s first Northrop Grumman Transport Layer plane: Falcon 9 launch scrubbed Oct 5, retry set for Oct 6

The fourth Tranche 1 mission (T1TL-A), 21 Northrop Grumman-built data transport satellites, was set for a Falcon 9 liftoff from Vandenberg SLC-4E on Oct 5 but scrubbed shortly before liftoff on an automated abort. Per search-result summaries, the vehicle and satellites are reported in good health and a new attempt is planned for Oct 6 (window roughly 12:59–1:21 a.m. PT). It is the first Tranche 1 mission carrying Northrop satellites. Launch outcome not confirmed at compile time.

Oct 1L3Harris awarded $461.9M for commercial SATCOM connectivity on executive airlift aircraft

A Pentagon contract notice dated Oct 1 shows the Space Force awarded L3Harris $461.9 million for commercial satellite communications connectivity on executive airlift aircraft. The firm-fixed-price contract runs through Oct 31, 2028, with $62.3 million in FY2026 O&M funds obligated at award.

Oct 1Pentagon’s new Cyber Mastery Incentive Pay model has cyber troops bracing for pay cuts

DefenseScoop reports that the Cyber Mastery Incentive Pay program (C-MIP), announced in June and effective Oct 1, replaces existing skill-based pay with skill incentive pay and special duty assignment pay. Internal documents cited show monthly cuts of roughly $100 to $1,000 for some personnel, and the Army, a heavy contributor to the cyber force, is applying a temporary pay-protection policy to soften the impact. The Pentagon’s cyber policy chief Katie Sutton addressed the program, which it pitched as a “new era” for retaining cyber talent.

Sep 30White House nominates Lt. Gen. David Miller Jr. to lead U.S. Space Command

The Senate received the nomination Sept 29 and referred it to the Armed Services Committee. Miller, currently Space Force deputy chief of space operations for strategy, plans, programs and requirements and a former Space Operations Command commander, would succeed Gen. Stephen Whiting. If confirmed he would be the fourth SPACECOM commander since its 2019 reestablishment; Defense Daily notes he would oversee the command’s move to Huntsville.
