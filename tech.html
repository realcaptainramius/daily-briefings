Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

Oct 1NSA launches Post-Quantum Cryptography Resource Hub, restates 2027/2030 NSS milestones

NSA announced a new set of measures for protecting National Security Systems from quantum threats, anchored by a Post-Quantum Cryptography Resource Hub aimed at DoW, NSS operators, and the Defense Industrial Base. The messaging restates the existing timeline: all new commercial NSS must be able to support quantum-resistant algorithms starting in 2027, and legacy systems that can't be upgraded are to be phased out by 2030. NSA also names a second threat model alongside "harvest now, decrypt later" — "trust now, exploit later," which targets authentication assets such as code-signing keys, certificate authorities, device identities, and secure-boot chains. Separately, NSA published a rewritten QKD statement the same day that keeps its 2020 position against QKD for NSS. Practical takeaway: expect customers to start asking about PQC readiness of identity/signing infrastructure, not just data-in-transit encryption. (This is a reiteration of known dates, not a new deadline.)

Secureframe · ISI Defense
OngoingCNSA 2.0: network gear now, every new NSS acquisition by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support now; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027 — under three months out — with exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes, not a differentiator, and unlike the CMMC deadline above, nothing has paused this clock.

Emerging Tech Radar open-ended

Sep 29NATO publishes public summary of its Quantum Technology Roadmap

NATO released the public summary of a quantum roadmap its Allies approved July 30, covering quantum computing, sensing, and communications, with activities including use-case identification, pilots, standardization/interoperability, annual maturity assessments, and adoption of post-quantum cryptography standards. Named milestones include quantum-sensor sea trials in 2026–2027, an initial military-pilot report in early 2027, and a communications pilot linking NATO HQ in Brussels with SHAPE in Mons by end of 2027; public deadlines run through Q3 2029. Notably, commentary points out it sets no firm PQC migration date. Relevant for allied/coalition comms and interoperability requirements.

Sep 29University of Vienna reports first in-orbit operation of a programmable quantum photonic processor

A University of Vienna-led team posted a preprint describing a programmable photonic quantum processor operating on a nanosatellite (launched June 2025) — a 6-mode photonic circuit manipulating two photons, in a roughly 9.8 kg, 10 W payload. Detector failures, radiation damage, and sunlight noise limited usable measurement time to about 30 minutes per orbit, and no practical computing task or quantum advantage was shown. It's a preprint-stage technology demonstration, not a fielded capability, but a data point for on-board processing in non-terrestrial/SATCOM contexts.
