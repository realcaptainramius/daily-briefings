Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

Nov 9Carahsoft adds PQSecure PQC portfolio (and IonQ) to its public-sector catalog

Carahsoft announced it will distribute PQSecure Technologies’ post-quantum cryptography portfolio — PQC software libraries plus FPGA, ASIC and SoC hardware IP — to government buyers, and in a same-day release became IonQ’s public-sector distributor for quantum computing, networking, sensing and security products. Per the announcements, two PQSecure products (PQSecure-Agility and PQSecure-Lib) have been approved through the Department of War CIO’s ATLAS program, and the offerings are available on Carahsoft’s TIPS, OMNIA Partners and E&I contract vehicles. Practical takeaway: PQC components are increasingly orderable through standard federal reseller channels, following earlier 2026 Carahsoft additions of PQShield and pQCee. Dated Nov 9, the same day last week’s briefing was compiled, so it was not covered there.

OngoingCNSA 2.0: network gear now, every new NSS acquisition by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support now; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027 — about six and a half weeks out — with exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. Note that ML-KEM-768 (the parameter set in common browser hybrid key exchange) does not satisfy CNSA 2.0. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes, not a differentiator, and unlike the CMMC deadline above, nothing has paused this clock. Recent context: NIST IR 8547 (final, Oct 27) and the NSA/CISA/ASD vendor-question set (Nov 4), covered last week.

Nov 10SDA taps SpaceX to study putting Starlink-derived “Plaser” laser terminals on missile-tracking satellites

The Space Development Agency awarded SpaceX a roughly $9.9M contract (up to about $12.7M with options), signed Nov 9 and announced Nov 10, for a technical risk-reduction study, delivery of representative hardware, and integration support for SpaceX’s commercial Plaser optical terminal with missile warning/tracking satellites — SDA’s LEO Tracking Layer and Space Systems Command’s MEO tracking satellites. Work runs through October 2027. Reporting does not say whether Plaser would fly on Tracking Layer satellites or relay through another network, or whether it will be tested against SDA’s optical terminal standard — worth watching for anyone selling into the optical-crosslink supply chain, since a commercial terminal entering the architecture could pressure SDA-standard terminal vendors. This is an early-stage study, not a fielded capability.

Nov 13Commerce finalizes up to $100M CHIPS R&D award to Atom Computing

The Commerce Department’s CHIPS R&D Office finalized an award of up to $100M to neutral-atom quantum computing company Atom Computing, converting the May 21, 2026 letter of intent (one of nine LOIs totaling about $2.01B, with Commerce taking minority equity stakes) into a signed award. Funding targets hardware development and systems integration for machines that can control and validate tens of thousands of qubits. It follows Atom’s October selection as one of four companies advanced to Stage C of DARPA’s Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (alongside IBM, IonQ and Diraq). The $100M is a ceiling, not cash disbursed. Confirmed via NIST’s announcement and the company release carried by multiple outlets.
