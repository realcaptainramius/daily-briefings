Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

Sep 21 — 4 days outFIPS 140-2 sunset is now this week: certs flip to "Historical" status Monday

This coming Monday, every FIPS 140-2 validation certificate moves to Historical status in NIST's Cryptographic Module Validation Program. Fielded systems keep running, but Historical-status modules generally can't satisfy NIST SP 800-171 control 3.13.11 ("FIPS-validated cryptography") in a fresh C3PAO assessment — only FIPS 140-3 validated modules qualify for new procurement going forward. That collides with CMMC 2.0 Level 2 third-party assessments starting seven weeks later, on November 10, creating a tight fall compliance window. If any product in your stack is still leaning on a FIPS 140-2 cert as its compliance story, that gap is now live, not hypothetical.

Secure Controls Framework · CMMC Ready Now
RFI due Sep 27DoD wants software-only "bridge" encryption to buy time before full PQC migration

An August 27 DoD RFI is seeking data packet-protection software that can be dropped in without touching existing hardware — no chip, radio, or platform replacement — while the department works toward its post-quantum cryptography deadlines (PQC support across all systems by Dec 31, 2030; PQC in use department-wide by Dec 31, 2031). Requirements include DoD-approved MFA, exclusion of unauthorized parties, and DoD retaining full control of decryption keys. Industry responses are due September 27. This is a near-term, software-defined opportunity for anyone selling crypto-agility or key-management capability into DoD networks — a lower-lift sale than a hardware refresh, and the government is explicitly asking for it right now.

DefenseScoop · Federal News Network
OngoingCNSA 2.0 timeline: network gear this year, all new NSS acquisitions by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support this year; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes in roughly three months, not a future differentiator.

5G / Open RAN & Tactical Networking

DoD's open-architecture 5G push, tactical-edge deployments, and vendor moves.

RecentOCUDU Open RAN stack graduates into a Linux Foundation-hosted ecosystem with heavyweight backing

The Pentagon-seeded OCUDU open-source 5G/6G RAN project now has its own Linux Foundation-hosted OCUDU Ecosystem Foundation, and it's pulling in serious industry weight: Qualcomm and Intel have joined as Premier Members alongside AMD, AT&T, Ericsson, Nokia, NVIDIA, Samsung, SoftBank, Verizon, and original builders DeepSig and Software Radio Systems. General members include Cisco, Red Hat, Booz Allen, T-Mobile, and Airspan, with 21 new global member organizations added since launch. This matters because it signals OCUDU is moving from a DoD-funded proof of concept toward a real multi-vendor standard — the kind of open reference stack that becomes an RFP requirement rather than a nice-to-have. If your SATCOM/tactical-comms line touches the RAN layer, this is the ecosystem to be plugged into.

Linux Foundation · Linux Foundation (Qualcomm)
FY2026 NDAANDAA mandates a prioritized base list for private 5G investment and tactical-radio 5G evaluation reporting

The FY2026 NDAA puts legislative teeth behind DoD's private 5G push: it requires a prioritized list of bases for private 5G investment and mandates reporting on evaluations of 5G-based tactical radios. That's a funding and accountability signal on top of the existing DoD Private 5G Deployment Strategy — it converts "we're rolling this out" into a base-by-base list Congress will track. Worth watching which installations land on that priority list, since that's effectively a near-term procurement roadmap.

Sep 14Major SATCOM operators pivot from selling capacity to multi-domain defense + AI integration

At World Space Business Week in Paris, leaders from Viasat, SpaceX, Eutelsat, Telesat, and Space42 described a shift away from the traditional "sell capacity" model toward multi-domain, defense-integrated, AI-layered offerings — per Space42's Karim Michel Sabbagh, the industry is moving from "quite monolithic" to covering "multiple domains, vertical and horizontal integration, and integration of launch-based capabilities." For a SATCOM seller into USAF/USSF, this is a signal that your competitors and prime partners increasingly expect to bundle AI-enabled network management and cross-domain (space/air/ground) capability into the pitch, not just bandwidth.

Sep 1Telesat Government Solutions and Cailabs team up on secure optical (laser) connectivity for government missions

Telesat Government Solutions and French photonics firm Cailabs announced a collaboration on secure optical/laser connectivity aimed at government and defense missions. It lands alongside a rougher story for military laser comms: the Space Development Agency's Tranche 1 satellite-to-satellite laser links are running at least three months behind schedule for its Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture mesh network, per a recent GAO report. Net read: demand for optical inter-satellite links is real and growing (lower latency, harder to intercept/jam than RF), but the government program of record is struggling with integration timelines — which is exactly the kind of gap commercial optical-comms partnerships are stepping into.
