Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

CorrectionCMMC Level 2 third-party enforcement is suspended, not landing Nov 10 as previously flagged

Last week's briefing flagged November 10 as a hard CMMC Level 2 third-party-assessment tripwire — that's no longer the state of play, and it's worth correcting directly. DoD suspended CMMC Phase 2 back on July 13 via a memorandum tied to Secretary Hegseth's Acquisition Transformation Strategy, pausing the mandatory C3PAO third-party assessment requirement pending a Reform Task Force review. DoD reissued DFARS Class Deviation 2026-O0025 (Revision 3) on September 3, directing contracting officers to strip third-party CMMC requirements from solicitations and permit Level 1/Level 2 self-assessment in the interim. The Task Force's 60-day review closed September 11; its recommendations are now with the DoW CIO, unpublished, with a public report expected late September into October. FIPS-140-3/CNSA 2.0 crypto obligations and a current SPRS self-assessment score remain live requirements today — but the hard third-party-assessment deadline is off the table for now, so don't let a pitch or a prospect's urgency lean on it.

DefenseScoop · Federal News Network
OngoingCNSA 2.0: network gear now, every new NSS acquisition by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support now; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027 — roughly three months out — with exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes, not a differentiator, and unlike the CMMC deadline above, nothing has paused this clock.

Emerging Tech Radar open-ended

Whatever else is genuinely new and relevant this week — not a fixed topic list.

Sep 24Infleqtion entangles 30 logical qubits on commercial neutral-atom hardware

At Quantum World Congress, Infleqtion announced its Sqale neutral-atom quantum computer entangled 30 logical qubits (encoded across 80 physical atoms) in a single circuit connecting all 30, using roughly 1,000 physical quantum operations — hitting a 2026 roadmap commitment and making Infleqtion the first neutral-atom company to clear 30 logical qubits on a commercial system. It's a research milestone, not a fielded capability, but it's a useful real-world data point for gauging how fast "cryptographically relevant quantum computer" timelines are actually moving against the PQC-migration deadlines DoD is planning around above; Infleqtion's own roadmap targets 100 logical qubits by 2028.

Sep 25Creotech Quantum clears EU milestone, moves proprietary QKD system toward commercial rollout

The European Commission signed off on the results of Creotech Quantum's EC-backed eCAUSIS project, clearing the way for the Polish firm to commercialize a BB84-protocol quantum key distribution (QKD) system aimed at government and critical-infrastructure buyers, with a stated production goal of up to 1,000 modules/year scaling toward 10,000/year within two years. Worth flagging as a distinct track from the PQC-algorithm migration covered above: QKD does physical-layer key exchange over dedicated fiber/optical links rather than swapping math on existing gear, so it's complementary rather than competing, and mostly relevant to fixed point-to-point links rather than the mobile/tactical use cases in your SATCOM world — but worth knowing the vendor landscape as some federal buyers start asking about it alongside PQC.
