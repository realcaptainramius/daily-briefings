Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

Oct 27NIST finalizes IR 8547, the PQC transition timeline

NIST published the final version of NIST IR 8547, Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards, dated October 27, 2026 — nearly two years after the November 2024 initial public draft. Per NIST's announcement and secondary summaries, it plans to deprecate quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms (RSA, ECC) after 2030 and disallow them after 2035, aligns high-value and high-impact federal systems with Executive Order 14412 and OMB M-26-15 (post-quantum key establishment by 2030, signatures by 2031), and accommodates hybrid classical/PQC implementations. These deadlines are now effectively vendor requirements for anyone selling crypto-bearing products to federal customers. Details come from secondary summaries; the full PDF was not reviewed, so confirm specifics against NIST before quoting. Separately, OMB M-26-15 agency migration plans were due around October 22; no source reports whether agencies met that date (unconfirmed).

Secureframe · ISI Defense
OngoingCNSA 2.0: network gear now, every new NSS acquisition by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support now; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027 — about 8 weeks out — with exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes, not a differentiator, and unlike the CMMC deadline above, nothing has paused this clock.

Oct 27IonQ gets $7.5M AFRL Phase 2 allocation to add free-space optics to Rome, NY quantum network

IonQ announced a $7.5M Phase 2 follow-on from the Air Force Research Laboratory adding free-space optical links (line-of-sight entanglement distribution with automatic pointing, acquisition and tracking), quantum frequency converters and O-band-compatible networking to its Rome, NY testbed. Per coverage, this is not new money: it falls within the originally announced $21.1M collaboration (January 2025), and the system is a research platform, not meant for operational fielding yet. Confirmed across IonQ’s release as reported by multiple outlets; last week’s briefing held this back because the date was then unconfirmed.
