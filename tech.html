Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

Today, Sep 21FIPS 140-2 officially sunsets to "Historical" status

As of today, every FIPS 140-2 validation certificate moves to Historical status under NIST's Cryptographic Module Validation Program. Nothing breaks in place — systems already running FIPS 140-2 modules keep operating — but CMVP guidance is clear that agencies "should not include" Historical-status modules in new procurements. Only FIPS 140-3 validated modules qualify going forward. The next tripwire lands in 50 days: CMMC Level 2 third-party assessment enforcement begins November 10, and FIPS-validated cryptography is a prerequisite for Control 3.13.11. If any product in your stack is still quoting a FIPS 140-2 cert as its compliance story, that's a live gap on any new federal deal starting today, not a future one — worth a direct question to your vendors this week.

CMMC Ready Now · SafeLogic
Aug 28, responses due Sep 27DoD RFI seeks software-only, drop-in quantum-safe encryption

The Pentagon issued a request for information seeking interim, software-only cryptographic solutions that require zero hardware changes — no new chips, crypto cards, radios, or HSMs — to protect data during the PQC transition. The RFI ties directly to DoD's PQC Strategy timeline (systems support PQC by Dec. 31, 2030; full PQC use by Dec. 31, 2031) and explicitly frames this as a bridge while hardware refreshes catch up. For sellers, this is a concrete signal that DoD wants software-defined, "drop-in" crypto-agility products it can layer onto fielded gear right now, not just next-gen hardware roadmaps — and industry responses are due September 27, so this is a live window to get a position on record.

DefenseScoop
OngoingCNSA 2.0: network gear now, every new NSS acquisition by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support now; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027 — about three months out — with exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes, not a differentiator, and the clock is now inside one quarter.

5G / Open RAN & Tactical Networking

DoD's open-architecture 5G push, tactical-edge deployments, and vendor moves.

Sep 4 (updated Sep 16)NIST ships new release of its 5G/O-RAN open-source testbed automation tool

NIST's Communications Technology Laboratory updated its 5G Open-Source Testbed Automation Tool to support the latest releases of OCUDU and Duranta — the two open-source RAN stacks anchoring DoD's Open RAN push — plus a new GNU Radio/ZeroMQ-based channel emulator that lets researchers run reproducible, multi-cell 5G experiments without specialized radio hardware. This is infrastructure-for-the-infrastructure: it lowers the bar for any lab, integrator, or university partner to stand up a credible OCUDU testbed without buying a radio range, which widens the pool of people who can credibly demo against the DoD reference stack. If your team is evaluating or integrating against OCUDU, this tool is now the fastest path to a repeatable test environment.

NIST
ContextOCUDU governance has moved to the Linux Foundation, with major vendors now "premier partners"

Earlier this year the OCUDU project — originally a DoD FutureG Office award to DeepSig and Software Radio Systems — was folded into a new Linux Foundation "OCUDU Ecosystem Foundation" to steward the open-source 5G/early-6G RAN stack going forward. Premier partners now listed include AMD, AT&T, DeepSig, Ericsson, Nokia, Nvidia, SoftBank, SRS, and Verizon — meaning the incumbents DoD originally wanted to break vendor lock-in from (Ericsson, Nokia) are now co-governing the open alternative. Worth tracking for what it signals about how "open" OCUDU stays as it scales, and worth knowing before you position a bid as the open-RAN disruptor play.

For SATCOM/tactical-comms sellers, base-level private 5G is the near-term, budgeted demand signal sitting right next to the longer-horizon OCUDU/tactical-edge story — two different sales motions on the same wave.

Sep 20Space-based laser comms cross from demo to operational deployment at 10 Gbps

Industry assessments this week confirm space-based optical inter-satellite links (OISL) are now shipping in standardized, commercial hardware rather than one-off NASA demos, with space-qualified terminals hitting 10 Gbps across LEO constellations and deep-space relays. The pitch for defense buyers: optical's narrow beamwidth resists intercept, jamming, and spoofing far better than RF, and terminals now fit smallsat SWaP budgets. The catch is weather — clouds and moisture still knock out optical downlinks, so operators are pairing laser crosslinks with backup RF, which is exactly the kind of hybrid architecture a SATCOM/tactical-comms integrator should be pricing into any proliferated-LEO or ground-relay bid from here forward.
