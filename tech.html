Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

UpcomingCivilian-agency PQC migration plans due to OMB/ONCD around October 22 — days away

Per secondary reporting, OMB Memorandum M-26-15 (issued June 24, following Executive Order 14412 of June 22) requires each executive department and agency to submit a PQC migration plan to OMB and the Office of the National Cyber Director within 120 days — roughly October 22, 2026 — about three days from this briefing. Plans are to cover risk-based prioritization, phased timelines, automated cryptographic inventory, crypto-agility architecture, third-party coordination, and funding. The memo does not apply to National Security Systems, which stay on the CNSA 2.0 track below. Unconfirmed against the memo text itself: all sources found are vendor/consultancy blogs and cite the date as approximate, so verify before relying on the exact day. Expect civilian-agency customers to be asking vendors for crypto inventories and PQC roadmaps right now. No new NIST, NSA, or CISA PQC publications were verified for Oct 12–19; NSA's Oct 1 PQC Resource Hub announcement remains the latest, and NIST's FIPS 206 (FN-DSA) and FIPS 207 (HQC) drafts could not be confirmed as published.

Secureframe · ISI Defense
OngoingCNSA 2.0: network gear now, every new NSS acquisition by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support now; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027 — about 10 weeks out — with exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes, not a differentiator, and unlike the CMMC deadline above, nothing has paused this clock.

Emerging Tech Radar open-ended

Whatever else is genuinely new and relevant this week — not a fixed topic list.

Oct 12IonQ to acquire zero-trust vendor Elisity in all-stock deal worth up to $1.3B

IonQ announced an agreement to acquire Elisity, which sells identity-based microsegmentation controlling which users, devices, workloads, and AI agents can communicate on enterprise networks. Up to $150 million of the value is performance-based earnouts; close is expected in Q4 pending regulatory approval. IonQ says the combined roadmap would add cryptographic inventory and risk data to Elisity's platform, tying zero trust to post-quantum and QKD work. Deal terms and customer figures are company-reported. Relevant as a signal that quantum-hardware vendors are moving into network-security products aimed at the same buyers as PQC migration.

IonQ · Quantum Computing Report · GovConWire
Oct 14India's DRDO tests quantum key distribution on a live weapons-test range network

DRDO said it configured and tested a QKD platform on the live network of the dynamic test ranges at the Proof & Experimental Establishment, Chandipur, to protect telemetry from artillery and armoured-weapon trials; Bengaluru-based Taqbit Labs is the production partner. DRDO claims eavesdropping detection and “unconditional” security; coverage is largely rewrites of DRDO's statement, with no independent test data published. An earlier July 2026 fibre trial reportedly achieved roughly 5 kbps secure key rate over 100 km. QKD only distributes keys and does not replace PQC for most deployments, but it shows foreign militaries fielding quantum-secured links.
