Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

Sep 21 (5 days out)FIPS 140-2 sunsets to "Historical" status — hard cutover for new procurement

On September 21, every FIPS 140-2 validation certificate moves to Historical status with NIST's Cryptographic Module Validation Program. Existing systems keep running, but agencies "should not include" Historical-status modules in new procurements going forward — only FIPS 140-3 validated modules qualify. For DoD contractors under CMMC/DFARS, this has direct audit teeth: CMMC Level 2 enforcement follows seven weeks later, on November 10. If any product in your stack is still quoting a FIPS 140-2 cert as its compliance story, that's now a live gap for any new federal deal, not a future one.

SafeLogic · CMMC Ready Now
OngoingCNSA 2.0 timeline: network gear this year, all new NSS acquisitions by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) is the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support this year; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027, with full exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. Translation: if you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support isn't a differentiator anymore, it's table stakes starting in about three and a half months.

QuSecure
ContextDoD still says no to QKD — PQC algorithms, not quantum hardware, are the sanctioned path

Worth knowing when a vendor pitches quantum key distribution hardware: DoD's CIO has stated the department does not currently allow QKD (or similar quantum-hardware key distribution) for confidentiality, authenticity, or integrity purposes. The sanctioned path is algorithmic — CNSA 2.0/PQC software and firmware upgrades, not photon-based key exchange. QuSecure's QuProtect platform reaching TRL-7 at the Army's Project Convergence Capstone 6 is a useful real-world proof point of that algorithmic approach maturing in an operational setting.

5G / Open RAN & Tactical Networking

DoD's open-architecture 5G push, tactical-edge deployments, and vendor moves.

RecentPentagon-backed open-source RAN stack ("OCUDU") is now a working codebase

Under a National Spectrum Consortium contract, DeepSig and Software Radio Systems have spent the past several months building out OCUDU, an open-source 5G/6G software stack the Pentagon wants to publish for broader use. It's reportedly now a functioning, downloadable RAN stack, not just a spec on paper. DoD's rationale for open RAN generally: security through transparency, letting military engineers actually inspect and patch the stack rather than trust a vendor's black box. If your SATCOM/tactical comms offering touches the RAN layer, this is the reference architecture to know.

DefenseScoop
May 2026Lockheed & Nokia Federal ship modular 5G.MIL for the tactical edge

Lockheed Martin and Nokia Federal Solutions integrated carrier-grade 5G with Lockheed's 5G.MIL capability inside the Department of War's CMOSS open-architecture framework, aimed at secure, resilient, high-speed comms at the tactical edge with rapid upgrade paths across vehicles and platforms. Consistent with the DoD budget directive to roll out private 5G/Open RAN across all DoD bases globally — this is one of the concrete integrator plays landing inside that mandate.

Late 2026 (planned)Lockheed, IBM & Swiss defense procurement stand up a quantum computing hub

IBM and Lockheed Martin, working with Swiss defense agency armasuisse, are building a national quantum innovation hub at ETH Zurich, anchored by Switzerland's first on-premises IBM Quantum System Two, to be installed at the Swiss National Supercomputing Centre in Lugano by late 2026. Notable as an allied-nation, defense-funded quantum computing (not just quantum-safe crypto) investment — a different track from the PQC migration work above, and one to watch if quantum compute starts showing up in allied targeting/logistics modeling work relevant to coalition ops.
