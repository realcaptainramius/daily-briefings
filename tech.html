Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST/NSA standards, CNSA 2.0 and CMMC deadlines, and PQC vendor/DoD adoption.

Nov 12GSA opens market-research RFI on PQC product readiness (responses due Dec 7)

GSA’s Federal Acquisition Service posted a request for information asking industry about commercial products and services that support NIST-approved PQC (FIPS 203/204/205) — covering FIPS validation status, implementation maturity, procurement accessibility through GSA vehicles, interoperability, operational impacts and migration support. The RFI cites Executive Order 14412 and OMB Memorandum M-26-15 (agency migration execution) as its drivers. It is market research only: GSA says it will not assign work under it and has made no commitment to a follow-on solicitation. Responses are due December 7, 2026 per the SAM.gov listing (one aggregator shows Dec 8; treat the SAM.gov date as controlling). The posting date (Nov 12) comes from contract-listing aggregators and fell inside last week’s window but was not surfaced then. Practical takeaway: vendors and resellers with PQC-capable products have a low-cost opportunity to shape how GSA packages PQC buying for civilian agencies.

No change found as of Nov 23: CMMC Phase 2 (third-party assessment) remains suspended since July 13, and searches this week again found no public release of the Task Force report delivered to the DoW CIO around September 11. Crowell & Moring · Inside Government Contracts
OngoingCNSA 2.0: network gear now, every new NSS acquisition by Jan 1, 2027

NSA's CNSA 2.0 suite (ML-KEM-1024 for key establishment, ML-DSA-87 for signatures, plus AES-256/SHA-384-512) remains the mandated quantum-resistant algorithm set for National Security Systems. VPNs, firewalls, routers, and switches used in NSS need CNSA 2.0 support now; every new NSS acquisition must be CNSA 2.0-compliant starting January 1, 2027 — about five and a half weeks out — with exclusive use across operating systems, custom applications, and cloud services required by 2033. Note that ML-KEM-768 (the parameter set in common browser hybrid key exchange) does not satisfy CNSA 2.0. If you're bidding network or SATCOM gear into an NSS-designated environment, CNSA 2.0 support is table stakes, not a differentiator, and unlike the CMMC deadline above, nothing has paused this clock. Also still pending: FIPS 206 (FN-DSA/Falcon) has not been published as a final standard; no NIST finalization was found this week.

5G / Open RAN & Tactical Networking

DoD's open-architecture 5G push, tactical-edge deployments, and vendor moves.

Nov 18NATO publishes summary of its first Alliance-wide Next Generation Communication Networks strategy

NATO released a public summary of its first Alliance-wide Next Generation Communication Networks (NGCN) strategy, which defence ministers endorsed in June 2026 (NATO’s official-texts page for the summary carries an Oct 8 date; the public announcement was Nov 18). NGCN is defined broadly: 5G/6G and future cellular, next-gen SATCOM, optical communications, wireless line-of-sight and beyond-line-of-sight, digital radio and wired services. The strategy takes a mission-driven, multi-technology approach that explicitly allows public, private and hybrid networks — including commercial cellular and SATCOM — with security, resilience and cost trade-offs weighed per mission, and it flags untrusted vendors as a risk to address. It positions these networks as force multipliers for counter-drone sensor fusion and AI-enabled command and situational awareness. Next steps: an NGCN Innovation Range co-led by Finland and Sweden where industry can test in operational conditions, and an NGCN Roadmap now in development. Relevance: a policy-level signal that hybrid commercial/military 5G-plus-SATCOM architectures are the Alliance direction — useful framing for coalition and EUCOM-facing opportunities. The full strategy is not public.

Nov 16IBM, NVIDIA and Fermilab plan a modular quantum–classical codesign platform

Announced at the start of SC26, the three organizations plan a codesign platform at Fermilab pairing IBM quantum computers (including Nighthawk-processor systems) with NVIDIA DGX B200 systems, aimed at quantum error-correction codes, real-time decoding, and hybrid quantum-classical workflows for high-energy and nuclear physics. NVIDIA concurrently released a CUDA-Q decoding algorithm it says runs up to 13,000× faster for error-correction research, and the partners announced Slurm integrations to slot quantum processors into supercomputer job scheduling. These are announcement-stage plans with vendor-supplied performance figures and no stated operational date. Relevance: tight GPU–QPU coupling and HPC-scheduler integration are becoming the default architecture for government quantum programs — expect future lab and DoD quantum procurements to bundle classical accelerated compute.
