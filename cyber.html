Breaches & Incidents

Nov 12TSMC says employee data leaked via an outside cloud provider; seller claims 46,000 records

TSMC said data on some current and former employees appeared online. The data matched records held by an unnamed outside cloud provider, and TSMC found no compromise of its own systems. A seller claims 6.9GB and about 46,000 records, including names, national IDs, addresses and bank accounts, priced at 3 BTC with a one-week deadline. These are the seller’s claims; TSMC has not confirmed the count or the provider. Seen via search summaries, largely from aggregator sites.

Cybernews · Cyber Security News
Nov 11–12Attackers abuse cloud ScreenConnect at MSPs to push “SCKit” backdoor to 288 endpoints

Huntress reports that on Nov 11 attackers used ScreenConnect’s Run Command feature in cloud-hosted instances at six or more managed service providers to push rogue clients and an “SCKit” backdoor toolkit to 288 endpoints across 48 organizations within 90 minutes. The likely entry point was RMM+, a third-party ScreenConnect extension, and ConnectWise disabled five extensions across about 400 partner environments. Some outlets call SCKit worm-like, but Huntress and Blackpoint did not see it spread successfully to patched clients. Seen via search summaries.

Huntress · BleepingComputer · SecurityWeek
Nov 11Apify rotates secrets after internal-systems breach; Fullerton Health server for Singapore migrant-worker care plan breached

Web-scraping platform Apify said attackers had unauthorized access to internal systems on Nov 9–10. It rotated critical secrets, revoked some third-party OAuth connections including GitHub, and told users to reset 2FA. Apify says it found no sign account data was affected, while Risky Bulletin reportedly says customer accounts were accessed. In Singapore, Fullerton Health disclosed a breach, discovered Oct 31, of a server supporting the Ministry of Manpower’s Migrant Worker Primary Care Plan. Names, foreign ID numbers, birth dates and contact details may have been exposed, but no medical or financial data, and no count was given. Seen via search summaries only.

Vulnerabilities & Exploits

Nov 8–12N-able N-central zero-day exploited within hours of public PoC, even on latest hotfix

BleepingComputer reports researcher Sean Bales released “4EverAble2Shell” and “Agent2Shell” exploits claiming root on N-central appliances and SYSTEM on Windows agents. Attackers used them within hours to drop cryptominers on at least 21 systems, 19 of them on N-able’s latest hotfix. This follows the “Able2Shell” batch of alleged zero-days published Nov 8–9, after which N-able took hosted N-central offline on Nov 9 and shipped 2026.4 Hotfix 2 on Nov 10. Patch and CVE status for the new flaw is unconfirmed. Seen via search summaries only.

BleepingComputer · Tenable FAQ
Nov 10–12Critical Apache httpd and Red Hat Quay flaws fixed; unpatched Windows “SignDrop” privilege-escalation PoC published

Apache HTTP Server 2.4.71, released Nov 11, fixes a mod_http2 race condition (reported as CVE-2026-119618, CVSS 9.8 per Red Hat) allowing unauthenticated remote code execution on 2.4.55–2.4.70 with HTTP/2 enabled. No in-the-wild exploitation is reported. Red Hat Quay flaws reported as CVE-2026-123710 (CVSS 9.8) and CVE-2026-110697 (CVSS 9.9) can be chained to read, overwrite and inject images across a registry; fixes were merged Nov 11 and Quay.io is fixed, but sources disagree on self-hosted fix status. Researcher Abdelhamid Naceri published a Windows local privilege escalation to SYSTEM, dubbed SignDrop, on Nov 10; it has no CVE or patch and Microsoft has not confirmed it. No CISA KEV additions dated Nov 11–13 were found. CVE IDs come from search summaries.

Nov 10–11Barracuda ESG flaws now exploited; Fortinet, Citrix, Ivanti and Git ship patches

SecurityWeek reports attackers are chaining two Barracuda Email Security Gateway flaws patched Nov 2 (reported as CVE-2026-116606, auth bypass, and CVE-2026-111560, command injection) to gain root; CISA’s Nov 10 KEV batch reportedly includes two Barracuda flaws plus Openwave Webtop. Fortinet published details of AI-assisted fixes it shipped in late October, with a SecurityWeek headline citing nearly 100 vulnerabilities; no exploitation was reported. Citrix and Ivanti patched critical and high-severity flaws, and Git fixed six flaws including a recursive-clone code-execution bug. No KEV additions dated Nov 11–12 were found. Seen via search summaries and headlines only; CVE IDs should be checked against vendor advisories.

Nov 9–10November Patch Tuesday fixes exploited Outlook flaw; Chrome zero-day patched; CISA adds batch to KEV

Microsoft’s Nov 10 Patch Tuesday includes one exploited zero-day, an Outlook spoofing flaw (reported as CVE-2026-124194) that can leak NTLMv2 hashes; outlets report wildly different totals for the release (about 1,093 to 1,259 CVEs). A SonicWall SonicOS SSLVPN flaw was reportedly added Nov 6. CVE numbers and dates come from search summaries and differ between sources.

Nov 12US moves to seize $1.2B of North Korea’s Binance theft and disrupts “FaceStealer” deepfake scheme

DOJ obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from the roughly $6 billion Binance theft around Oct 29, which the FBI attributes to North Korea’s TraderTraitor. Agents also seized cloud infrastructure behind “FaceStealer,” attributed to APT38/BlueNoroff, which allegedly stole more than $400 million over 18 months by impersonating victims with a library of 900+ secretly recorded webcam videos. The FBI published an advisory on Nov 12. No charges were announced, and the funds are held under a seizure order, which does not transfer ownership. Seen via search summaries.

Nov 9Wiz: attacker abused Qubole’s trusted AWS access to reach customer accounts

Wiz, working with Sentra and Mandiant, reported that a threat actor used IAM credentials tied to an AWS account associated with data-lake platform Qubole (owned by Idera) between Oct 14 and Oct 26 to assume customer roles that trusted Qubole, then read Lambda environment variables, Secrets Manager and SSM Parameter Store values, stealing customers’ AWS keys and Entra ID and Okta client secrets, with some opportunistic S3 downloads. A separate Oct 8–11 wave used customers’ own access keys set up for Qubole. How the credentials were obtained has not been disclosed; AWS said its services operated as designed. Seen via search summaries of the Wiz post and press coverage.
