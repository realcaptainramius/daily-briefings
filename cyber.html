Breaches & Incidents

Nov 11Maersk confirms breach of a legacy system; WhiteArmor group demands $5M

Maersk disclosed on Nov 11 a breach of a legacy system inherited through an acquisition, saying it is confined to one local system, has not affected other systems or customer API/EDI connections, and that findings so far strongly suggest no sensitive data was disclosed. The WhiteArmor extortion group claims about 100GB from the LF Logistics platform Maersk bought in 2022 and is demanding $5 million in bitcoin; Maersk has not tied the incident to WhiteArmor, and reports conflict on what was taken. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 9Nitori says up to 37.62M customer records may have leaked; Vanderbilt University hit by cyberattack

Japanese retailer Nitori Holdings disclosed on Nov 9 unauthorized access confirmed Nov 6, with an intrusion in mid-to-late October that may have exposed email and home addresses of members and online buyers; it says passwords and card data were not compromised, and whether 37.62 million counts people or records is unclear. Also on Nov 9, Vanderbilt University told its community to shut down or disconnect devices during a cybersecurity incident; a group calling itself “BYOD” listed the university on its leak site claiming research data and more than 1 million SSNs, which the university has not confirmed. Seen via search summaries only.

Nov 4–7Crédit Mutuel Arkéa loses roughly €100M in attack via a service provider

French bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa acknowledged on Nov 4 a cyberattack that reporting says entered through an unnamed service provider’s platform and moved funds through the bank’s own internal account; the bank says no customer account was debited. Amounts differ: about €100 million (the bank says only the order of magnitude is right), €118 million per a cited investigator, and more than $133 million per blockchain investigator ZachXBT. Nov 9Sega says 2.2M newsletter email addresses exposed in breach at mail provider Arara

Sega disclosed that a breach at Arara Inc., the Japanese email-delivery provider it uses for newsletters, exposed 2,196,044 email address records, 92,273 of which also held newsletter-delivery data. Sega says names, birth dates, addresses, phone numbers, card details and passwords were not included and Sega Accounts are unaffected; Arara reported the unauthorized access on Nov 1 and notified Sega on Nov 3. Sega warns of phishing risk and says this is separate from a Nov 2 Sega Fave incident. Seen via search summaries of press coverage.

Vulnerabilities & Exploits

Nov 10–11Barracuda ESG flaws now exploited; Fortinet, Citrix, Ivanti and Git ship patches

SecurityWeek reports attackers are chaining two Barracuda Email Security Gateway flaws patched Nov 2 (reported as CVE-2026-116606, auth bypass, and CVE-2026-111560, command injection) to gain root; CISA’s Nov 10 KEV batch reportedly includes two Barracuda flaws plus Openwave Webtop. Fortinet published details of AI-assisted fixes it shipped in late October, with a SecurityWeek headline citing nearly 100 vulnerabilities; no exploitation was reported. Citrix and Ivanti patched critical and high-severity flaws, and Git fixed six flaws including a recursive-clone code-execution bug. No KEV additions dated Nov 11–12 were found. Seen via search summaries and headlines only; CVE IDs should be checked against vendor advisories.

SecurityWeek · CISA (Nov 10) · SecurityWeek · SecurityWeek · The Hacker News
Nov 9–10November Patch Tuesday fixes exploited Outlook flaw; Chrome zero-day patched; CISA adds batch to KEV

Microsoft’s Nov 10 Patch Tuesday includes one exploited zero-day, an Outlook spoofing flaw (reported as CVE-2026-124194) that can leak NTLMv2 hashes; outlets report wildly different totals for the release (about 1,093 to 1,259 CVEs). Google fixed an exploited Chrome XML use-after-free (reported as CVE-2026-125350) on Nov 9, counted by outlets as the eighth Chrome zero-day of 2026. Reports say CISA added the Outlook and Chrome flaws, a Cisco BroadWorks XML parser flaw and others to KEV on Nov 10, and on Nov 9 or 10 added GPUStack, FreeSWITCH, IBM Aspera Faspex and the OpenSSH “SSHole” flaw (CVE-2026-123158, musl-based systems such as Alpine with PAM disabled), which watchTowr said on Nov 6 was being exploited by botnets and cryptominers. A SonicWall SonicOS SSLVPN flaw was reportedly added Nov 6. CVE numbers and dates come from search summaries and differ between sources.

Barracuda (Nov 11)
Nov 9Wiz: attacker abused Qubole’s trusted AWS access to reach customer accounts

Wiz, working with Sentra and Mandiant, reported that a threat actor used IAM credentials tied to an AWS account associated with data-lake platform Qubole (owned by Idera) between Oct 14 and Oct 26 to assume customer roles that trusted Qubole, then read Lambda environment variables, Secrets Manager and SSM Parameter Store values, stealing customers’ AWS keys and Entra ID and Okta client secrets, with some opportunistic S3 downloads. A separate Oct 8–11 wave used customers’ own access keys set up for Qubole. How the credentials were obtained has not been disclosed; AWS said its services operated as designed. Seen via search summaries of the Wiz post and press coverage.
