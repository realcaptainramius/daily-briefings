Breaches & Incidents

Nov 9Sega says 2.2M newsletter email addresses exposed in breach at mail provider Arara

Sega disclosed that a breach at Arara Inc., the Japanese email-delivery provider it uses for newsletters, exposed 2,196,044 email address records, 92,273 of which also held newsletter-delivery data. Sega says names, birth dates, addresses, phone numbers, card details and passwords were not included and Sega Accounts are unaffected; Arara reported the unauthorized access on Nov 1 and notified Sega on Nov 3. Sega warns of phishing risk and says this is separate from a Nov 2 Sega Fave incident. Seen via search summaries of press coverage.

Nov 10Reported: Fireworks AI discloses breach via third-party cloud provider; Philippine SEC confirms breach

BleepingComputer reports, per a search summary, that AI inference startup Fireworks AI disclosed attackers reached its development machines after breaching a third-party cloud provider, accessing resource metadata, billing information and incomplete model weights and datasets for some customers. Separately, the Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission confirmed a breach of its digital platforms, without giving a count of affected users and saying no passwords leaked. Neither disclosure was reviewed directly.

Vulnerabilities & Exploits

Nov 9–10November Patch Tuesday fixes exploited Outlook flaw; Chrome zero-day patched; CISA adds batch to KEV

Microsoft’s Nov 10 Patch Tuesday includes one exploited zero-day, an Outlook spoofing flaw (reported as CVE-2026-124194) that can leak NTLMv2 hashes; outlets report wildly different totals for the release (about 1,093 to 1,259 CVEs). Google fixed an exploited Chrome XML use-after-free (reported as CVE-2026-125350) on Nov 9, counted by outlets as the eighth Chrome zero-day of 2026. Reports say CISA added the Outlook and Chrome flaws, a Cisco BroadWorks XML parser flaw and others to KEV on Nov 10, and on Nov 9 or 10 added GPUStack, FreeSWITCH, IBM Aspera Faspex and the OpenSSH “SSHole” flaw (CVE-2026-123158, musl-based systems such as Alpine with PAM disabled), which watchTowr said on Nov 6 was being exploited by botnets and cryptominers. A SonicWall SonicOS SSLVPN flaw was reportedly added Nov 6. CVE numbers and dates come from search summaries and differ between sources.

Cloud & Infrastructure Security

Nov 9Wiz: attacker abused Qubole’s trusted AWS access to reach customer accounts

Wiz, working with Sentra and Mandiant, reported that a threat actor used IAM credentials tied to an AWS account associated with data-lake platform Qubole (owned by Idera) between Oct 14 and Oct 26 to assume customer roles that trusted Qubole, then read Lambda environment variables, Secrets Manager and SSM Parameter Store values, stealing customers’ AWS keys and Entra ID and Okta client secrets, with some opportunistic S3 downloads. A separate Oct 8–11 wave used customers’ own access keys set up for Qubole. How the credentials were obtained has not been disclosed; AWS said its services operated as designed. Seen via search summaries of the Wiz post and press coverage.
