Breaches & Incidents

Sep 14–16CenterPoint Energy confirms breach of up to ~7.5M customer records

The Houston-based utility confirmed in a Sept 14 SEC 8-K filing that an unauthorized third party accessed customer personal information through an external-facing system. A threat actor claims to have pulled 7.49M records via an API allegedly lacking adequate authentication and rate limiting; CenterPoint hasn't confirmed that exact figure but has engaged outside responders and hardened the affected system. Multiple proposed class-action suits have already been filed.

Vulnerabilities & Exploits

Sep 15–17Two critical Cisco bugs under active exploitation — ISE (CVSS 10.0) and Secure Email Gateway

CVE-2026-76460, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) flaw in Cisco ISE, lets unauthenticated attackers bypass the web management interface and run commands as root; Cisco confirms active exploitation. A second, related bug, CVE-2026-76461 in Cisco Secure Email Gateway, allows root-level arbitrary command execution and is also being actively exploited — CISA pushed agencies to patch it before Sept 17. If you run either product, this is drop-everything patching, not routine maintenance.

Cyber Press · Qualys ThreatPROTECT
Sep 8Microsoft's largest-ever Patch Tuesday: 974 CVEs, including 2 exploited zero-days

September's Patch Tuesday set a record at 974 CVEs (113 critical, 20 wormable). Two are already under active exploitation: CVE-2026-85880 and CVE-2026-81963, both Windows elevation-of-privilege bugs (ALPC and Update Stack respectively) that let an attacker who already has a foothold escalate to SYSTEM. Both require existing access, so pair patching with a look at initial-access controls, not just the patch itself.

Sep (ongoing)Energy/utilities sector hit in 66% of tracked APT campaigns; usual state-linked crew active

A new industry roundup finds energy and utilities the most-targeted sector across observed APT campaigns this period, with China-linked Mustang Panda, North Korea's Lazarus Group, and Russia's Sandworm among the most active groups, spanning attacks across 18 countries. Separately, researchers flagged APT28 weaponizing a newly disclosed vulnerability within 24 hours of disclosure — a reminder that patch-cycle SLAs matter more than they used to.
