Breaches & Incidents

Oct 1AI agent used two Zammad zero-days to breach the Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure

DIVD disclosed that a Sept 21 intrusion exploited two Zammad ticketing-system zero-days, CVE-2026-102489 (CVSS 9.4, unauthenticated RCE and session leak) and CVE-2026-102490 (CVSS 9.4, local privilege escalation to root), chained within seconds. The attacker moved to other services and exfiltrated data, though network segmentation stopped deeper access. Scripts contained notes in which the agent justified its own actions, which DIVD says points to an agentic AI attacker. DIVD urges all Zammad users to update to version 7 or take instances offline.

Sep 29Pentagon DMDC breach exposed unencrypted records of more than 3 million people, including Social Security numbers

The Defense Manpower Data Center said a vulnerability in a file-sharing system, found July 16 and patched, gave a small number of unauthorized users access to a server holding unencrypted personal data between October 2025 and July 2026. About 2.76 million living and 294,000 deceased individuals are affected; exposed fields include names, dates of birth, SSNs and job specialties. The notification letter is dated Sept 18 and the incident became public Sept 24; counts circulated Sept 29–30. DoD reports no indication of misuse and offers one year of credit monitoring. No attribution has been reported in the sources reviewed.

Vulnerabilities & Exploits

Oct 3–4Citrix patches exploited NetScaler SAML zero-day CVE-2026-88779; CISA adds it to KEV

Citrix published bulletin CTX697174 on Oct 3 and released NetScaler ADC/Gateway 14.1-73.41 and 13.1-64.28 for CVE-2026-88779 (CVSS v4 8.7), a memory-buffer flaw that affects appliances configured for SAML authentication and can let an unauthenticated attacker cause persistent denial of service. Citrix confirmed targeted attacks on unmitigated deployments; researchers are investigating possible remote code execution. CISA added it to KEV on Oct 4 with a federal due date of Oct 7.

BleepingComputer · The Hacker News · CISA
Oct 2CISA adds the exploited Zammad flaws (CVE-2026-102489, CVE-2026-102490) to KEV

CISA added both Zammad bugs behind the DIVD breach to KEV on Oct 2: CVE-2026-102489 (session fixation, CVSS v4 9.4) and CVE-2026-102490 (improper privilege management, local escalation to root). Federal agencies must mitigate or discontinue use by Oct 5 under BOD 26-04; the fix for CVE-2026-102489 is Zammad 7.2.0.

CISA · WindowsForum
Oct 1Fortinet FortiMail zero-day CVE-2026-104286 exploited in the wild; CISA adds to KEV

Fortinet published advisory FG-IR-26-175 on Oct 1 and CISA added CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8) to KEV the same day. The path-traversal/NULL-byte flaw lets an unauthenticated attacker write arbitrary files via crafted HTTP(S) requests. Affected: FortiMail 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.2.0–7.2.9. Reporting at the time said no patch was available; Fortinet advised disabling identity-based encryption or restricting the management interface. Check Fortinet for current fix status.

Threat Actor & APT Activity

Oct 1Proofpoint: China-aligned TA419 impersonates AI policy figures to phish U.S. AI experts

Proofpoint reported (published Thursday, Oct 1) that TA419 targeted experts on AI regulation, export controls and national AI strategy at U.S. think tanks, universities and legal organizations. Beginning July 8 the group posed as Lynne Parker and then Heidi Crebo-Rediker, inviting targets to a fictitious AI policy advisory committee or a purported Senate Foreign Relations report, then steering repliers through a Cloudflare Turnstile check to an OneDrive adversary-in-the-middle credential-phishing page. Activity likely supports Chinese intelligence collection on U.S. AI policy.

Oct 1Police seize KillSec infrastructure and arrest suspected 16-year-old leader

Spanish police announced the arrest of a 16-year-old Romanian national suspected of administering KillSec, part of a Sept 30 Europe-wide operation led by Hamburg authorities with Europol that also produced arrests in the UK and Romania. Five central servers and the leak site holding at least 110TB of stolen data were seized. The teen-run extortion group is said to have compromised about 500 organizations since 2024; a developer was identified but not arrested.
